## Source Boundary

This document preserves the pre-chapter showrunner development supplied with Chapter Seventeen, *The Physician*. It is admitted as **Exploratory** and does not promote titles, membership in the Twelve, wounds, identities, Testament language, or seven-book trajectories to Canon.

The source describes “Books One through Fifteen” as awakening. In context, this appears to mean **Chapters One through Fifteen** of Book One. That wording is normalized here as a chapter-threshold proposal; the original wording remains preserved in the source artifact.

## Formation Threshold

**Proposed structural movement:**

- **Chapters One through Fifteen:** Awakening
- **Chapter Sixteen and beyond:** Formation

The transition should allow readers to love the fellowship before the greatest battles begin. The Twelve are not assembled as a collection of experts. Their formation reveals Christ redeeming broken image-bearers.

## Governing Showrunner Principle

> Every member of the Twelve represents one wound Christ intends to heal.

A role or occupation may describe what a character does. The wound identifies where the character's formation must become costly, relational, and spiritually meaningful.

## Chapter Seventeen Application

*The Physician* dramatizes the proposed wound of playing God through Gabriel Alvarez's work at the BioContinuity Institute. His first movement is not conversion or appointment. It is the beginning of a question:

- Is memory continuity personal survival?
- Is postponing death equivalent to defeating it?
- Has medicine crossed from healing embodied persons into redesigning humanity?
- Has Gabriel mistaken technical power for his calling?

The chapter preserves a deliberate gate: the Testament reveals **a title**, not Gabriel's name, and Michael answers, “Not yet.”

## Continuity and Theology Review Flags

1. **Continuity leadership:** The governed Master Project Document associates Continuity with Dr. Miriam Kaine. Gabriel Alvarez's role as “one of its chief architects” must be reconciled with Kaine's established development role.
2. **The Twelve:** The wound grid proposes a membership structure that intersects the unresolved virtue/calling architecture and hidden-twelfth question.
3. **Testament behavior:** Fragment XVII and the emergence of a title touch the unresolved rules and limits of *The Testament of Light*.
4. **Scripture:** The supplied verse is Mark 8:36 in KJV wording and should be identified as such in editorial review if retained.
5. **Theological precision:** The elderly woman's phrase “I already have one waiting” should be reviewed for whether it could imply a presently existing replacement body rather than future bodily resurrection.
6. **Comparative references:** The source names *The Lord of the Rings*, *The Chosen*, and *The Stand* as craft comparisons. These are development references, not public positioning or canon.

## Historical Consequences

Book One gains a proposed transition from awakening to formation, and the Twelve gain a unifying design centered on wounds rather than professional competence.

## Narrative Payoffs

Each gathering can become both an expansion of the fellowship and the beginning of a distinct redemptive arc. Gabriel's awakening connects Continuity, embodiment, death, resurrection, and the limits of technical medicine.

## Production Notes

Track each wound as an arc question rather than a label. Show it through choices, relationships, temptation, consequence, and repentance. Do not make every wound equally visible at introduction, and do not let prophetic titles erase ordinary personhood.
