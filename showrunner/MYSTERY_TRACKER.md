## Mystery Rules

1. A mystery must have an answer before the manuscript depends on precise foreshadowing.
2. Concealment must arise from viewpoint, danger, ignorance, or moral choice—not authorial evasion.
3. Every clue must remain truthful after the reveal.
4. Red herrings may misdirect interpretation but may not falsify reality.
5. Revelation must deepen character and meaning, not merely provide information.
6. Book Seven reserves must be decided early enough that Books One–Six can protect them.

## Founder Decision Queue

## Historical Consequences

The tracker creates an honest record of what the studio knows, what characters know, and what readers are allowed to infer at each stage.

## Narrative Payoffs

Mysteries can reward rereading because their clues remain coherent and gain meaning after revelation.

## Production Notes

For adaptation, add episode-level clue locations and visual or sonic motifs only after the corresponding novel beats are stable.
