## Core Creative Values

1. **Christ remains central.** The saga’s meaning and destination cannot be detached from Him.
2. **Story before message.** Truth must become dramatically credible through people, conflict, choice, and consequence.
3. **Truth before spectacle.** A visually powerful moment cannot excuse theological, emotional, historical, or technical dishonesty.
4. **Characters before ideology.** Readers encounter ideas through recognizable human beings rather than mouthpieces.
5. **Redemption is costly.** Change carries sacrifice, restitution, surrender, and transformed practice.
6. **Hope is realistic, not sentimental.** Hope faces suffering truthfully and rests in God’s promises rather than denial.
7. **Technology is morally consequential, not inherently evil.** Tools and systems carry benefits, incentives, limits, and effects shaped by human worship and choice.
8. **Every character possesses genuine moral agency.** Systems exert pressure without erasing responsibility.
9. **Scripture illuminates rather than interrupts the narrative.** Biblical truth should deepen the story without becoming an exposition shortcut.
10. **The Kingdom of God is the true protagonist of the saga.** Individual arcs matter within the larger movement from false kingdoms toward the Kingdom that cannot be shaken.

## Application

When values appear to conflict, protect truth, Christ-centered purpose, character integrity, and the governed canon before convenience or spectacle. Apply the Three Horizons—Story, Civilization, and Audience—and record unresolved conflicts for Founder review.
