## Storytelling Rules

### Rule 1 — Characters before ideology

Readers remember people, not lectures. Ideas must become visible through desire, pressure, choice, consequence, relationship, and change.

### Rule 2 — Demonstration before explanation

The Gospel should be demonstrated before it is explained. Faith becomes credible through costly action, repentance, forgiveness, hospitality, courage, worship, service, and sacrifice.

### Rule 3 — Technology is not the villain

Technology is morally consequential, but human choices, institutions, incentives, worship, and uses of power determine its role. Show genuine benefits as well as costs.

### Rule 4 — Every antagonist believes they are saving something

Solomon, ORACLE, and future opponents must retain comprehensible motives. No major antagonist is evil merely for the sake of evil.

### Rule 5 — Every major character must change

If a major character ends a book exactly as they began, determine whether the arc is incomplete. Change may include formation, repentance, hardening, betrayal, surrender, courage, or clearer allegiance.

### Rule 6 — The Three Tests

Every chapter must satisfy at least one test:

1. **Story:** Does this meaningfully advance the narrative?
2. **Character:** Does this reveal or permanently change someone?
3. **Kingdom:** Does this deepen understanding of God’s Kingdom?

The strongest chapters satisfy all three. If a scene satisfies none, it probably does not belong.

### Rule 7 — Resolution with consequence

Each book should resolve its central conflict while opening a meaningful path toward the next installment. An ending should create consequence, not merely advertise a sequel.

## Relationship to Existing Creative Law

The private Creative Constitution remains authoritative and applies the Three Horizons—Story, Civilization, Audience—plus the political, human, and spiritual historical method. The Three Tests in this proposal are an additional scene-level tool, not a replacement.

## Historical Consequences

The writers’ room gains concise scene and arc tests that translate the project’s larger principles into daily drafting decisions.

## Narrative Payoffs

Characters, Kingdom meaning, and plot movement reinforce one another instead of competing for page space.

## Production Notes

Apply the Three Tests at outline and revision stages. Record exceptions deliberately; do not preserve a scene solely because its information might be useful elsewhere.
