# Character Arc Framework

**Canon Status:** Exploratory
**Version:** 0.1
**Last Reviewed:** July 2026
**Depends On:** `canon/CONSTITUTION.md`; `canon/SERIES_BIBLE.md`; `showrunner/CONTINUITY_LEDGER.md`; private archive `05-characters/CHARACTER_DOSSIERS.md`

## Purpose

This framework keeps a civilizational epic emotionally human. It is a design and review instrument, not a source of canon.

## The Arc Card

Complete one card per principal character:

| Field | Required question |
|---|---|
| Identity | Who does the character believe they are? Who has authority to name them? |
| Want | What concrete outcome do they pursue now? |
| Need | What truth, repentance, formation, or surrender do they require? |
| Wound | What loss, guilt, fear, or lie constrains them? |
| Gift | What can they give that the system cannot manufacture? |
| False good | What plausible good justifies their wrong path? |
| Pressure | Which institution, relationship, or event makes avoidance costly? |
| Choice | What costly action reveals the arc? |
| Cost | What cannot be recovered after that choice? |
| Witness | Who sees the change, and who refuses to see it? |
| End state | What has become more true, and what remains unresolved? |

## Arc Movement

Use this sequence flexibly: **self-understanding → pressure → compromise or refusal → consequence → truth-telling → costly action → transformed practice.** A conversion is not complete when a character says the right thing; it becomes credible when their habits, loyalties, and use of power change.

## Series Tracking

For each book or season, record:

- external objective;
- internal lie under pressure;
- relationship that tests the lie;
- political, human, and spiritual turn;
- knowledge gained and withheld;
- promise planted;
- consequence carried forward;
- status: canon fact, provisional beat, or exploratory proposal.

## Arc Guardrails

- Do not make characters mouth the Constitution instead of embodying it.
- Do not make the Fellowship morally frictionless.
- Do not confuse trauma with destiny or technology with moral agency.
- Antagonists must make the strongest honest case for their chosen good.
- Faith appears through action, restraint, sacrifice, confession, forgiveness, hospitality, work, worship, and courage.
- Track what each character knows before and after every major scene.

## Review Questions

1. What does this character want today?
2. What are they afraid the truth will require?
3. What choice costs them something observable?
4. How does the choice alter another person's future?
5. Does the arc deepen the central conflict rather than merely decorate it?
6. Does the ending preserve responsibility, grace, and mystery?

## Historical Consequences

A shared arc language prevents the large world from swallowing the people who must carry its meaning.

## Narrative Payoffs

Small choices can accumulate into credible repentance, courage, betrayal, reconciliation, and restoration across seven books.

## Production Notes

Maintain one arc card per principal in `series-bible/characters/`. Update the Continuity Ledger whenever a choice changes knowledge, relationship, injury, possession, allegiance, or future obligation.
