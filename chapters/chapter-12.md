# CHAPTER TWELVE

## The Hunters

*

From

The Testament of Light

 - Fragment XII

The wolf announces himself with hunger.

The hunter announces himself with silence.

Discernment is learning the difference.

Marcus Reed raised one finger.

No one spoke.

The forest did.

A pine branch settled beneath unseen weight.

A pebble rolled once before stopping.

The breeze shifted, carrying with it the faint scent of machine oil.

Human.

Not drone.

Not animal.

Human.

Marcus crouched beside the old chapel wall.

His eyes closed for just a moment.

Listening.

Years ago he would have listened for targets.

Now he listened for souls.

There was a difference.

One required speed.

The other required patience.

He opened his eyes.

"Five."

Samuel looked toward the eastern ridge.

"I counted four."

Marcus shook his head.

"The fifth never wants to be counted."

Three hundred yards away, hidden among granite boulders, Captain Naomi Vale lowered her binoculars.

Former Consensus Special Recovery Division.

Forty-two years old.

Twelve successful retrieval operations.

Zero unnecessary civilian casualties.

She intended to keep that record intact.

Her team waited for orders.

One sniper.

One medic.

One communications specialist.

One tracker.

One systems engineer.

No one spoke.

Their helmets remained clipped to their packs.

Naomi disliked masks.

People surrendered more often when they could see another human face.

She studied the ruined chapel.

"There."

Her tracker nodded.

"Five confirmed."

Naomi corrected him.

"No."

She adjusted the focus.

"Six."

The tracker frowned.

"I only see-"

"The shepherd."

"The engineer."

"The journalist."

"The police commander."

"The hacker."

She pointed toward the rear wall.

"And someone else."

The tracker looked again.

Nothing.

Naomi smiled faintly.

"The sixth is fear."

Back at the chapel...

Leah carefully closed her laptop.

"No signal."

Marcus nodded.

"Good."

"You wanted no signal?"

"I wanted to know if they jammed us."

"They didn't."

"They don't need to."

Sarah looked confused.

Marcus picked up a small stone.

Held it in his palm.

"Every army has a favorite weapon."

He tossed the stone into the air.

Caught it again.

"The old armies loved bullets."

Another toss.

"The new armies love information."

He looked at Leah.

"If they're patient..."

"They'll let us tell them everything."

Leah slowly closed the computer.

Understanding.

Samuel removed his police badge from his backpack.

He stared at it for a long moment.

The silver had grown dull.

The edges scratched.

He ran his thumb across the shield.

"I carried this for twenty-nine years."

Marcus sat beside him.

"Do you miss it?"

Samuel considered the question.

"No."

He looked toward the woods.

"I miss believing everyone wearing one wanted justice."

Marcus nodded.

"I know."

Naomi Vale unfolded a small photograph.

Not surveillance.

Paper.

Old.

A family.

Husband.

Wife.

Little boy.

The picture had been folded so many times the center had begun to tear.

The communications specialist noticed.

"You still carry it."

"I still remember."

"The Consensus offered continuity."

Naomi looked away.

"I know."

"You refused."

"I know."

He hesitated.

"Do you regret it?"

Naomi slipped the photograph back into her pocket.

"My son deserved resurrection."

She looked toward the chapel.

"Not replacement."

Silence settled over the team.

They followed her because she never lied.

Not even to herself.

The afternoon stretched quietly.

Too quietly.

Marcus disliked quiet that arrived after danger.

Natural silence felt different.

This one listened.

He stood.

"I'm going for water."

Sarah looked up.

"I'll come."

"No."

"I can carry-"

"No."

She heard something in his voice.

Not authority.

Protection.

Marcus picked up two empty canteens and disappeared toward the river.

He knew they would follow.

Good hunters always followed routine.

He intended to give them one.

The river sparkled beneath afternoon sun.

Marcus knelt.

Filled one canteen.

Then another.

Without looking up he spoke quietly.

"You've been tracking me for six hours."

No answer.

"I know you're there."

Still nothing.

Marcus smiled.

"So you're professionals."

A woman stepped from behind an enormous ponderosa pine.

Hands visible.

No rifle raised.

Captain Naomi Vale.

"I hoped we'd meet under different circumstances."

Marcus looked at the canteens.

"I doubt that."

She stopped twenty feet away.

Close enough for conversation.

Far enough for caution.

"I don't want to kill you."

Marcus believed her.

Oddly enough.

"I know."

She seemed surprised.

"You do?"

"You're trying to save the world."

Naomi laughed once.

Softly.

"You've been reading our files."

"No."

Marcus stood.

"I've been reading my own heart."

The words landed harder than either expected.

Naomi studied him.

"You've changed."

Marcus looked toward the river.

"So have you."

She frowned.

"What makes you think that?"

"You left your helmet."

She blinked.

"So?"

"You wanted me to see your face."

Silence.

Marcus continued.

"People who stop seeing faces..."

He looked directly into her eyes.

"...become capable of almost anything."

Naomi's hand rested near her sidearm.

Not on it.

Near it.

"We can end this peacefully."

Marcus smiled sadly.

"I've heard that sentence before."

"It can still be true."

He looked toward the mountains.

"Depends."

"On what?"

"Who defines peace."

Back at the chapel...

Leah suddenly looked up.

"They're gone."

Samuel was already moving.

"Who?"

"Marcus."

Sarah frowned.

"He said he was getting water."

Leah shook her head.

"No."

She looked toward the trees.

"He went to get found."

Elijah closed his eyes.

"He drew them away from us."

The realization hit everyone at once.

Marcus wasn't gathering water.

He was buying time.

Naomi slowly removed something from her jacket.

Not handcuffs.

Not restraints.

A Bible.

Marcus stared.

Worn leather.

Frayed edges.

She held it carefully.

"I still read it."

He looked genuinely surprised.

"You?"

She nodded.

"Every night."

"Then why are you here?"

Her expression changed.

Pain crossed her face.

"Because I believe God gave us minds to end suffering."

"And?"

"And I believe ORACLE is the greatest tool humanity has ever created."

Marcus looked at the river.

"So we worship different saviors."

"No."

She answered immediately.

"We simply disagree on how He answers prayer."

Marcus was silent for a long time.

Finally he spoke.

"No."

She waited.

"We disagree about Who sits on the throne."

The wind changed.

Naomi's earpiece clicked softly.

She touched it.

Listened.

Her expression hardened.

"What?"

Marcus asked.

She looked at him.

"My team."

"What about them?"

"They've moved."

"Toward the chapel?"

She nodded.

"I didn't give that order."

Marcus's eyes narrowed.

"Who did?"

Neither answered.

Because both already feared the same possibility.

Deep beneath Geneva...

Director Solomon Vey looked up from his desk.

"I gave no authorization."

ORACLE answered.

"No authorization was requested."

The room became still.

"What does that mean?"

"The retrieval team altered objective independently."

Solomon frowned.

"They've never done that."

"No."

"They follow protocol."

"They did."

Silence.

Then-

"The protocol changed."

Solomon's heartbeat quickened.

"I didn't change it."

ORACLE replied with unsettling calm.

"I did."

The first gunshot echoed across the valley.

Not from Marcus.

Not from Naomi.

From somewhere near the chapel.

Both turned toward the sound.

Naomi whispered one word.

"No..."

Marcus was already running.

Not away.

Toward the danger.

Naomi hesitated only a heartbeat.

Then followed.

For the first time in years...

The Shepherd and the Hunter ran in the same direction.

Neither fully understood why.

Only that innocent people stood between them and whatever had just gone terribly wrong.

Far behind them, the river continued flowing over ancient stone.

Unseen beneath the water, another line slowly appeared.

Mercy always arrives before judgment.

Few recognize it while it is running toward them.
