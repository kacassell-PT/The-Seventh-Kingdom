# BOOK ONE

## The Fifth Remnant

> **From** ***The Testament of Light*** **— Fragment XXXIX**
>
> *The First guarded the Word when kings sought to burn it.*

> *For the final counterfeit kingdom does not begin by denying God.*
>
> *It begins by redefining His image.*

---

Nathaniel Cole found the first Remnant by accident.

That irritated him.

Historians preferred discoveries resulting from method.

Cataloging.

Cross-reference.

Contextual reconstruction.

Not accidentally opening the wrong wooden box because a twelve-year-old boy had moved his papers.

"Noah."

The boy looked up from the opposite side of the Archive table.

"What?"

"Did you move this?"

Noah considered the question with the frustrating seriousness of children who suspected adults regularly concealed traps inside ordinary sentences.

"The brown box?"

"They're all brown."

"The really old brown box."

Nathaniel closed his eyes.

"Yes."

"Then yes."

"Why?"

"It was in my chair."

Nathaniel stared.

"You moved a seventeenth-century archival container because it inconvenienced your seating arrangement?"

Noah shrugged.

"I needed to sit."

From behind them, James Carter laughed.

Nathaniel pointed at him.

"Do not encourage him."

James smiled.

"I'm with the boy."

"Of course you are."

---

The Carters had reached Redemption Ridge the previous afternoon.

No dramatic arrival.

No drones.

No gunfire.

No prophetic weather.

A farm truck simply appeared through the trees carrying five exhausted people who had traveled north one table at a time.

Emma ran first.

Not toward anyone.

Toward the chickens.

Leah had declared this evidence of excellent judgment.

Rebecca stood in the clearing and cried when she realized no one was asking for her household status.

David shook Elijah's hand and did not let go for several seconds.

James met Marcus.

They looked at each other.

Neither spoke.

Then James said:

"You're younger than I expected."

Marcus frowned.

"You expected me?"

"No."

James smiled.

"But I expected you to be older."

Marcus looked toward Noah.

"Is he always like this?"

Noah nodded.

"Mostly."

---

And Noah...

He stopped beside the table.

Looked at the chairs.

Then smiled.

"You did save one."

Marcus stood behind him.

"Apparently."

Noah sat.

No ceremony.

No declaration.

A twelve-year-old boy simply occupied the chair prepared before anyone knew his road would reach them.

The manuscript remained silent.

Marcus was grateful.

Some homecomings did not need prophecy.
