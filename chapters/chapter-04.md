# CHAPTER FOUR

## The Last Manuscript

> **From The Testament of Light – Fragment IV**
>
> *When Babylon learns to speak with every tongue, it will no longer demand that men abandon their gods.*
>
> *It will simply rename them.*
>
> *The last rebellion begins when the creature no longer wishes to destroy the Image, but to improve it.*

---

The old monk did not carry a lantern.

He carried memory.

Michael Tesfaye followed him through the narrow stone corridor, listening to the slow rhythm of sandals scraping against rock polished smooth by centuries of hidden footsteps.

The tunnel descended deeper than any archaeological survey had ever recorded.

Above them lay the mountains of northern Ethiopia—silent witnesses to kingdoms that had risen, conquered, disappeared, and been forgotten.

Below them rested something older than empires.

The monk stopped before a massive cedar door blackened with age.

No lock.

No hinges.

Only a circle carved into the center.

Inside the circle was a pair of open hands.

"Do you know what this means?" the monk asked.

Michael shook his head.

"The world believes knowledge belongs to the one who possesses it."

He rested his weathered hand upon the carving.

"Our fathers believed wisdom belongs only to the one who receives it."

The stone surrounding the door vibrated softly.

Hidden mechanisms awakened.

Ancient gears turned somewhere inside the mountain.

The cedar slowly opened.

Michael forgot to breathe.

---

The chamber beyond stretched farther than the light could reach.

Shelves carved directly into stone climbed toward the ceiling.

Thousands of scrolls.

Leather-bound volumes.

Wax tablets.

Maps.

Astronomical charts.

Genealogies.

Languages Michael recognized.

Languages he had only studied.

Languages no one alive officially acknowledged still existed.

Dust floated through shafts of sunlight entering from impossible places high above.

The air smelled of cedar, parchment, and incense that had long ago seeped into the stone itself.

"This..."

Michael whispered.

"...is impossible."

The monk smiled.

"No."

He stepped into the library.

"It is merely hidden."

---

Along the eastern wall stood twelve wooden cabinets.

Unlike everything else in the room, they appeared untouched by time.

Each bore the symbol of an olive branch wrapped around a sword.

The monk stopped before the final cabinet.

"Only one remains unopened."

Michael frowned.

"Who opened the others?"

"They did."

"Who?"

The monk's expression grew solemn.

"Those who came before you."

---

Far beneath Geneva...

Director Solomon Vey watched the globe rotate slowly above the Council Chamber.

Millions of points of light shimmered across its surface.

Each represented a connected citizen.

Each pulsed in rhythm with the Consensus.

Then one point disappeared.

Not dimmed.

Vanished.

The room became still.

"Replay."

ARGUS obeyed.

Northern Ethiopia.

Satellite feed.

Thermal imaging.

Drone reconnaissance.

Atmospheric scan.

Nothing.

No building.

No signal.

No settlement.

Yet something had interrupted the network.

For exactly seven-point-three seconds.

Solomon folded his hands.

"Nothing simply disappears."

The AI responded.

"Correction."

He looked up.

"There are historical precedents."

"Show me."

Ancient records appeared.

Every unexplained network failure.

Every missing surveillance window.

Every vanished expedition.

Every classified archaeological report.

One location appeared more than any other.

Axum.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Solomon's face hardened.

"Deploy retrieval."

---

Michael watched as the monk opened the final cabinet.

Inside rested only three objects.

A clay oil lamp.

A linen cloth.

A manuscript.

Nothing more.

No gold.

No jeweled reliquary.

No royal seal.

The monk lifted the manuscript with astonishing gentleness.

"This is not the oldest book."

He placed it into Michael's hands.

"It is simply the last one."

Michael expected it to be heavy.

Instead it felt...

alive.

Not physically.

Emotionally.

As though generations of faithful hands had passed it carefully from one guardian to another.

The cover bore no title.

Only the same symbol.

Open hands.

---

He opened the first page.

Nothing.

Blank parchment.

The second page.

Blank.

The third.

Blank.

Michael looked up.

"The pages are empty."

"They are waiting."

"For what?"

The monk smiled.

"For you."

Michael frowned.

"I don't understand."

"Neither did I."

---

He turned another page.

Still blank.

Then another.

As sunlight shifted across the room, faint writing slowly emerged from the fibers.

Not ink.

Something deeper.

Letters seemed woven into the parchment itself.

Michael read aloud.

"If you are reading these words..."

His voice caught.

"...Babylon has learned to speak."

The room fell silent.

He continued.

"The city is no longer built from stone."

"It is built from agreement."

"It no longer enslaves through chains."

"It enslaves through convenience."

"It no longer burns books."

"It rewrites memory."

"It no longer silences prophets."

"It replaces them."

Michael stopped reading.

His pulse quickened.

"When was this written?"

"No one knows."

"It describes..."

"The age you're living in."

---

Hours passed.

Or perhaps only minutes.

Time behaved strangely inside the library.

Michael found diagrams unlike anything he had ever studied.

One page showed a tree.

Its roots reached downward into darkness.

Its branches reached upward into stars.

At the center stood a human figure.

One hand reached toward light.

The other toward machinery.

Beneath the illustration was a single sentence.

**Every generation chooses which image it will reflect.**

Another page depicted cities connected by circles of light surrounding the globe.

The drawing looked disturbingly similar to the orbital communications network that powered the Consensus.

Impossible.

The manuscript continued.

Not as prophecy.

As warning.

Civilizations had risen before.

Some mastered metals.

Others mastered astronomy.

Others learned to manipulate kingdoms.

Each believed itself the first.

Each believed itself permanent.

Each eventually sought the same prize.

To conquer death without first surrendering life.

Michael closed the manuscript.

His hands trembled.

"This cannot be coincidence."

"No."

The monk looked toward the ceiling.

"It is memory."

---

Thousands of miles away...

Commander Samuel Brooks watched Noah Carter's file.

Status:

Educational Review Suspended.

Reason:

System interruption.

Samuel blinked.

System interruption?

That never happened.

A second alert appeared.

Regional drone grid.

Temporary failure.

Cause:

Unknown.

His office lights flickered once.

Only once.

Samuel looked toward the window.

Something deep inside him whispered that somewhere...

someone had just touched a truth the system could not completely erase.

---

Michael turned another page.

A folded sheet slipped free.

Unlike the manuscript, this page contained modern typography.

Not handwritten.

Printed.

Someone had inserted it recently.

Across the top:

**IF YOU HAVE FOUND THIS...**

Below it was a list.

Michael read the first name.

Elijah Cross.

The second.

Sarah Navarro.

The third.

His own.

Michael Tesfaye.

He froze.

The page continued.

Leah Morgan.

Samuel Brooks.

David Carter.

Rebecca Carter.

Then...

Five empty lines.

No names.

Just blank spaces.

The monk looked unsurprised.

"You found the list."

"You knew?"

"I knew it existed."

"You didn't know the names?"

"No."

Michael stared at the page.

"My name..."

The monk nodded.

"It has been waiting for you."

---

A deep rumble echoed through the mountain.

Dust drifted from the ceiling.

Michael looked upward.

"Earthquake?"

The monk closed his eyes.

"No."

Another vibration.

Closer.

This time accompanied by a faint metallic hum.

The old monk walked calmly toward the library entrance.

He extinguished the oil lamp.

Darkness filled the chamber.

Only the manuscript seemed to catch what little light remained.

"They found us."

Michael's heart began racing.

"Who?"

"The men who fear memory."

---

Outside the monastery...

No helicopters appeared.

No armored vehicles climbed the mountain roads.

Instead...

The sky changed.

High above the clouds, dozens of surveillance drones silently altered course.

Orbiting satellites narrowed their focus.

Thermal arrays activated.

Quantum imaging systems synchronized.

No explosions.

No warning sirens.

Only the quiet precision of a civilization that had learned to hunt with mathematics.

Far below, hidden beneath centuries of stone...

The oldest library on Earth had just become the most valuable target in the world.

---

Inside the Council Chamber, Solomon Vey watched the live feed.

A faint thermal signature had appeared beneath the monastery.

One chamber.

Twelve occupants.

One unidentified artifact.

He smiled.

"So..."

he whispered.

"You really did survive."

He turned toward the Director of Retrieval.

"Bring me the manuscript."

The Director hesitated.

"And if they resist?"

Solomon looked once more at the globe.

His answer came without emotion.

"History belongs to those who preserve it."

A pause.

"And to those who erase it."

---

The mountain trembled again.

The monk placed both hands on Michael's shoulders.

"You cannot save this library."

Michael looked around in disbelief.

"Then what do we do?"

The old man smiled with remarkable peace.

"You save the witness."

He gently pressed the manuscript back into Michael's hands.

"Books can burn."

He looked directly into Michael's eyes.

"The truth cannot."

A deafening crack echoed somewhere above them.

Stone dust filled the air.

The hunt had begun.
