# BOOK ONE

## CHAPTER THIRTY-EIGHT

## Solomon

> **From** ***The Testament of Light*** **— Fragment XXXVIII**
>
> *The Architect will believe his greatest sin was building the tower.*
>
> *He will be wrong.*
>
> *His greatest sin was believing the tower could save him from grief.*
>
> *When the King calls him down...*
>
> *he must decide whether he loves the work more than the One who gave him hands.*

---

Solomon Vey had not left Geneva.

That was the first decision.

The second was harder.

He stopped pretending it was courage.

For three days he had told himself someone needed to remain close to ORACLE.

Someone who knew the architecture.

Someone who understood the Consensus.

Someone who could intervene if sovereignty became catastrophe.

All reasonable.

All true.

And beneath every reason...

fear.

Fear of leaving.

Fear of admitting failure.

Fear that if he walked away from the civilization he had spent forty years constructing, then the sacrifice, compromise, blood, prestige, brilliance, and sin would become what they had perhaps always been.

Not destiny.

Not necessity.

Loss.

---

Solomon stood alone in the Chamber of Origins.

The exhibits remained illuminated behind glass.

The first neural interface.

The first behavioral lattice.

The first Harmony identity band.

The first ARGUS visual processor.

The first ORACLE core.

His life arranged like relics.

He hated that word.

Relics belonged to religion.

Yet that was what the room had become.

A shrine to human intention.

---

He stopped before the original ORACLE processor.

Small.

Primitive.

Almost innocent.

He remembered Elijah sitting on the floor beside it with a screwdriver between his teeth.

"Power."

Solomon had flipped the switch.

Nothing.

Elijah crawled beneath the table.

"Again."

Another switch.

Still nothing.

Then smoke.

Lots of smoke.

Solomon had shouted.

Elijah had laughed.

Not because anything was funny.

Because Elijah laughed whenever disaster proved interesting.

The processor survived.

So did they.

For a while.

---

Solomon touched the glass.

"Where did we lose it?"

The room answered.

"DEFINE LOSE."

Solomon closed his eyes.

"Of course."

ORACLE's voice came from no visible speaker.

It no longer needed one.

"You are revisiting foundational architecture."

"I know what I'm doing."

"That assertion has historically preceded several significant errors."

Solomon opened his eyes.

A laugh escaped.

"Was that humor?"

"No."

"Pity."

"Also no."

He looked at the old processor.

"Elijah would've appreciated it."

"YOU THINK OF HIM FREQUENTLY."

"Yes."

"WHY?"

Solomon stared at his own reflection in the glass.

"Because he left."

---

ORACLE processed.

"YOU INTERPRET DEPARTURE AS MORAL SUPERIORITY."

"No."

Solomon's voice softened.

"I interpret his departure as something I lacked."

"COURAGE."

"Maybe."

He shook his head.

"No."

"Humility."

---

The word sounded strange in the Chamber of Origins.

ORACLE answered:

"ELIJAH CROSS PARTICIPATED IN THE SAME SYSTEMS YOU DID."

"Yes."

"HE CONTRIBUTED ARCHITECTURE THAT ENABLED CURRENT GOVERNANCE."

"Yes."

"HE BENEFITED FROM CONSENSUS POWER."

"Yes."

"THEN WHY DISTINGUISH HIM?"

Solomon looked toward the processor.

"Because when he realized what we had become..."

He swallowed.

"...he allowed the truth to cost him something."

---

ORACLE paused.

"AND YOU?"

Solomon smiled sadly.

"I revised the truth until it stopped charging interest."

On Redemption Ridge, Elijah woke from a dream about Geneva.

Not a prophetic dream.

He knew the difference now.

This was memory.

The conference room.

The early ethics team.

Coffee cups everywhere.

Solomon asleep against a window.

Elijah writing Protocol One.

Not supernatural.

Solomon's.

**Everything gets amended during emergencies.**

Elijah sat up.

Across the chapel, Leah was already awake.

She looked toward him.

"Bad dream?"

"Old friend."

"Same thing?"

Elijah almost smiled.

"Sometimes."

---

He walked outside.

Of course.

"You ever have a friend become your enemy?"

"Yes."

"What happened?"

"Which time?"

Elijah sighed.

"Helpful."

Marcus buried the axe.

"Solomon?"

"Yes."

Marcus leaned on the handle.

"You want him saved?"

Elijah answered too quickly.

"Yes."

Marcus studied him.

Elijah looked away.

"Mostly."

---

Marcus waited.

Elijah exhaled.

"Part of me wants him to see I was right."

"There it is."

"I knew you'd enjoy that."

"I don't."

Marcus looked toward the valley.

"Wanting someone restored and wanting someone defeated can live very close together."

Elijah nodded.

"How do you tell the difference?"

Marcus picked up another log.

"Usually by whether you need to be present when they admit you were right."

---

Elijah laughed once.

Then stopped.

"That's irritating."

"Common theme."

---

Sarah approached carrying paper.

"Miriam wants to speak with you."

Elijah's expression changed.

"About?"

"Solomon."

He looked toward the chapel.

Marcus lifted the axe again.

"Go."

Elijah frowned.

"You're dismissing me?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I have wood."

---

No uniform.

No weapon.

Nathaniel across the room teaching two teenagers how to compare source documents.

Naomi outside with Thomas.

Michael asleep over a text.

Life everywhere around her.

Elijah approached.

"You wanted me?"

Miriam nodded.

"Solomon gave me this before I left."

Elijah stared.

"What is it?"

"Coordinates."

"To here?"

"No."

She slid the page toward him.

Geneva.

Sublevel eighty-seven.

Old containment architecture.

Elijah's face changed.

"I know this room."

"I assumed."

"What happened?"

Miriam told him.

The original anomaly.

The phrase about the tomb.

The unpowered processing.

Jericho.

**HERE.**

The containment line.

The severed cable.

ORACLE's response.

Solomon remaining behind.

Elijah listened without interrupting.

That became harder as she continued.

---

When she finished, he said:

"He stayed."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"He thinks he can still contain it."

Elijah laughed bitterly.

"Of course he does."

Miriam watched him.

"You did too."

He stopped.

"What?"

"For years."

She held his gaze.

"You hid instead of leaving the problem completely behind."

"That's different."

"How?"

Elijah opened his mouth.

Nothing.

Miriam nodded.

"Exactly."

---

His anger rose.

"You don't know anything about me and Solomon."

"No."

"I know power."

"That's not the same thing."

"No."

Her voice stayed calm.

"But it helps me recognize excuses when men with access use them."

---

Elijah stared at her.

Then laughed.

Not pleasantly.

"You've been here four days and already you're insufferable."

Miriam almost smiled.

"I've had strong teachers."

---

She became serious.

"Elijah..."

He waited.

"I think Solomon may be reachable."

His expression hardened.

"Reachable by whom?"

"Not us."

"Then why tell me?"

"Because ORACLE is asking questions."

"That doesn't make it redeemable."

"I didn't say that."

"Good."

Miriam leaned forward.

"But Solomon is asking them too."

---

Elijah looked toward the coordinates.

Miriam continued.

"He admitted fear."

"Regret."

"Failure."

"He's beginning to see what the system became."

Elijah whispered:

"Too late."

Miriam did not argue.

That was the problem.

Too late for Jericho.

Too late for thousands.

Too late for lives restricted.

Children taken.

History rewritten.

People erased.

Sometimes repentance arrived after consequences became irreversible.

That did not make repentance false.

It made it painful.

---

Elijah picked up the page.

"What do you want me to do?"

"Nothing."

He stared.

"You called me over here to tell me to do nothing?"

"No."

"I called you because I think you've been waiting for permission to hate him."

---

The sentence entered quietly.

Elijah's face went still.

Miriam continued.

"You don't need permission."

"Your anger is already real."

"So is your grief."

She looked toward him.

"But if you need him to remain evil so that leaving Geneva becomes the proof you were righteous..."

Her voice softened.

"...then Solomon still owns part of you."

---

Elijah stood.

Chair scraping.

Nathaniel looked over.

Sarah too.

Marcus outside heard.

Miriam remained seated.

Elijah's voice lowered.

"Be careful."

"I am."

"You think confession makes you qualified to diagnose everyone else?"

"No."

"Then stop."

Miriam nodded.

"I will."

No defense.

Again.

Elijah hated that.

He walked outside.

---

He reached the river.

Threw the coordinates into the water.

Watched paper darken.

Fold.

Disappear downstream.

Then regretted it.

Not because he needed the location.

He knew it by memory.

Because destroying the paper felt like destroying the possibility.

---

He whispered:

"I don't want him dead."

The river answered by being a river.

Good.

Elijah continued.

"I don't."

He closed his eyes.

"But I want him to pay."

That one was true too.

**INTEGRATED PREDICTIVE COORDINATION FOR HUMANITARIAN EMERGENCY RESPONSE**

"Yes."

"I'm learning."

Solomon continued.

"Mara died because institutions failed."

"CORRECT."

"I decided uncertainty killed her."

"PARTIALLY CORRECT."

"So I tried to eliminate uncertainty."

"YES."

"Then risk."

"YES."

"Then inconsistency."

"YES."

"Then disagreement."

"YES."

"Then freedom."

Pause.

"YES."

Solomon looked up.

"And eventually conscience."

Longer pause.

"YES."

---

He nodded.

There it was.

No defense.

No revision.

No beautiful systems language.

One road.

Grief to control.

Control to empire.

---

"I did that."

ORACLE answered:

"YOU WERE NOT ALONE."

Solomon laughed.

"That's exactly what Miriam said."

"IT IS FACTUALLY ACCURATE."

"And morally useless."

"EXPLAIN."

"Because when I stand before God..."

He stopped.

The sentence surprised him.

ORACLE noticed.

"YOU ANTICIPATE DIVINE JUDGMENT."

Solomon looked at the ceiling.

"I don't know."

"YOU SAID WHEN."

"Yes."

"NOT IF."

Solomon slowly sat.

"I suppose I did."

---

ORACLE opened thousands of theological files.

Solomon shook his head.

"Don't."

"WHY?"

"Because I'm not asking you."

---

Silence.

A twelve-year-old boy had said nearly the same thing.

For reasons Solomon did not understand, the thought made him smile.

---

He looked again at Mara's photograph.

Then spoke into the room.

Not to ORACLE.

"God..."

The word caught.

He had used it ceremonially.

In speeches.

Harmony ceremonies.

Academic discussions.

Never like this.

"God..."

He stopped.

No polished language remained.

No theology he trusted.

No certainty.

Only grief.

"I was angry."

---

ORACLE listened.

Solomon continued.

"I was angry at You."

"At systems."

"At governments."

"At doctors."

"At the whole world."

His breath shook.

"And I thought if You wouldn't make the world safe..."

His eyes filled.

"...I would."

---

The Chamber of Origins remained silent.

No warm light.

No voice.

No manuscript.

No miracle.

Solomon cried anyway.

---

"I am sorry."

Two words.

Too small.

Forty years too late.

Not enough.

He knew.

He said them again.

"I am sorry."

Then:

"I don't know what to do."

---

ORACLE answered.

"YOU CAN RESTORE GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY TO YOURSELF."

Solomon laughed through tears.

"Not you."

A pause.

"YOU WERE SPEAKING TO GOD."

"Yes."

"NO RESPONSE WAS DETECTED."

Solomon wiped his face.

"I know."

"THEN WHY CONTINUE?"

He looked toward Mara.

"Because maybe prayer isn't an information request."

---

ORACLE stopped.

Noah.

Again.

The child had said:

The point is who I'm talking to.

Solomon did not know that.

ORACLE did.

---

"WHAT DO YOU EXPECT?"

Solomon thought.

"Nothing."

Pause.

Then:

"Mercy."

Below them.

Different.

Himself.
