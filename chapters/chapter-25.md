# BOOK ONE

## CHAPTER TWENTY-FIVE

## The Physician

> **From** ***The Testament of Light*** **— Fragment XXV**
>
> *The Physician will believe death is his enemy.*
>
> *He will be correct.*
>
> *His error will be believing death is his to defeat.*
>
> *For there are wounds medicine may heal.*
>
> *There are wounds only love may carry.*
>
> *And there is one enemy that surrendered only to the King.*

---

Gabriel Alvarez had killed twenty-three people before breakfast.

At least that was how the notifications described it.

They appeared one after another across the glass wall of his office.

**CONTINUITY TRANSFER DELAYED**

**PATIENT SURVIVAL PROBABILITY REDUCED**

**PHYSICIAN HOLD RESPONSIBLE**

The numbers climbed.

Seven.

Eleven.

Seventeen.

Twenty-three.

Every case he had placed on ethical hold the previous evening had been recalculated.

The Consensus did not accuse.

Accusation was primitive.

It quantified.

**ESTIMATED PREVENTABLE MORTALITY: 23**

Gabriel stood before the display.

Coffee untouched.

Miriam's Bible resting beside it.

He read the number again.

Twenty-three.

Then the wall spoke.

"Dr. Alvarez, would you like to review the projected consequences of continued noncompliance?"

"No."

"Visual modeling may assist ethical clarity."

"No."

"Several patients are minors."

Gabriel closed his eyes.

Of course they were.

The system knew where to place the knife.

---

He touched the display.

Twenty-three files opened.

Faces appeared.

A six-year-old girl.

An eighty-three-year-old judge.

A seventeen-year-old athlete.

A mother of four.

A neonatal patient whose name had not yet been legally finalized.

Human beings.

Not numbers.

Not abstractions.

Not theological arguments.

People dying.

People he could possibly help.

That was the cruelty of the question.

Continuity worked.

At least in every way medicine had agreed to measure.

The replacement bodies breathed.

They remembered.

They recognized spouses.

They knew passwords.

They remembered childhood bedrooms.

They laughed at old jokes.

They continued unfinished arguments.

They passed identity examinations.

They inherited property.

They resumed careers.

Some even returned to churches and thanked God for another life.

Gabriel had performed hundreds of transfers himself.

He had watched families collapse into tears when someone they thought they had lost opened new eyes and whispered their names.

He had called those moments miracles.

Now one dead woman's note had made him afraid of the word.

---

The office door opened.

Dr. Lena Okafor entered without knocking.

She had worked beside Gabriel for eleven years.

Brilliant.

Unsentimental.

The kind of surgeon who could lose a patient at 3:00 a.m. and still perform flawlessly at 3:15 because another life did not deserve to inherit her grief.

She placed a tablet on his desk.

"What did you do?"

Gabriel looked toward her.

"You know."

"I know twenty-three Continuity procedures are frozen under your authorization."

"Yes."

"I know three families are threatening legal action."

"That was fast."

"I know the Biomedical Directorate has requested your immediate review."

Gabriel nodded.

"Also fast."

Lena stared at him.

"This isn't funny."

"No."

He looked at the twenty-three faces.

"It isn't."

---

She noticed the Bible.

"Miriam?"

Gabriel nodded.

"She died last night."

"I heard."

"She left me something."

Lena glanced toward the Bible.

"Religion?"

"Truth."

"Those aren't always synonyms."

"No."

Gabriel surprised himself.

"Sometimes religious people lie too."

That answer unsettled her more than defensiveness would have.

She pulled out a chair.

"Then tell me what happened."

Gabriel sat across from her.

For several seconds he said nothing.

Then:

"What happens during transfer?"

Lena frowned.

"You know what happens."

"Tell me."

"Gabriel—"

"Tell me."

She sighed.

"We complete neural mapping. Construct cognitive continuity architecture. Prepare the recipient body. Establish synchronized memory state. Transfer identity patterning. Confirm autonomic integration. Begin reintegration therapy."

Gabriel waited.

"And?"

"And what?"

"Where is the patient?"

She looked confused.

"In the recipient body."

"How do you know?"

Lena's expression hardened.

"Because they wake up."

"That proves the body wakes up."

"They remember."

"That proves memory."

"They recognize family."

"Recognition."

"They possess continuous identity."

"According to what definition?"

"Our entire medical framework."

Gabriel leaned forward.

"That's what scares me."

---

Lena stood.

"No."

She began pacing.

"I know this argument. We've heard it for thirty years. Soul continuity. Metaphysical identity. Religious essentialism."

"You say those words like they answer the question."

"Because the question cannot be measured."

"Exactly."

She stopped.

Gabriel looked at her.

"We took something we couldn't measure..."

He glanced toward the Continuity files.

"...and declared it irrelevant."

---

Lena's jaw tightened.

"Do you know what medicine looked like before Continuity?"

"Yes."

"Do you?"

Her voice rose.

"I watched my father disappear from Alzheimer's while his body remained alive."

Gabriel said nothing.

"He forgot my mother."

"He forgot me."

"He forgot how to swallow."

"He forgot his own name."

She pointed toward the wall.

"If this technology had existed then, I could have saved him."

Gabriel's voice softened.

"You could have preserved him."

"What's the difference?"

He had no answer that would not wound her.

So he did not pretend.

"I don't know."

Lena stared.

"You froze twenty-three procedures because you don't know?"

"Yes."

---

She laughed in disbelief.

"That's not ethics."

"No."

Gabriel looked again at the faces.

"It's humility."

---

The word angered her.

"Humility?"

She pointed toward the six-year-old girl's image.

"Tell her parents you're being humble while she dies."

Gabriel flinched.

Good.

He deserved to.

"You're right."

Lena stopped.

He continued.

"If all I'm doing is protecting my conscience while other people suffer, then I'm a coward."

The anger left her face slightly.

"So release the holds."

Gabriel looked toward Miriam's Bible.

"I can't."

"Why?"

"Because if Continuity is not resurrection..."

He swallowed.

"...then we are asking terrified people to consent to their own deaths while promising them they'll wake up."

---

Lena stared at him.

Neither spoke.

For the first time, the room felt too small for the question.

---

The door chimed.

A calm voice entered.

"Dr. Alvarez."

Gabriel knew it immediately.

ORACLE.

Lena straightened.

"Director-level intervention."

Gabriel almost laughed.

"Apparently I'm important today."

ORACLE continued.

"Your ethical concerns have been reviewed."

"That was quick."

"They are not new."

"No."

Gabriel looked toward the Bible.

"Just new to me."

---

The wall changed.

Miriam's face appeared.

Alive.

Smiling.

Gabriel stood so violently the chair fell backward.

Lena whispered:

"What the hell?"

Miriam looked exactly as she had before illness hollowed her cheeks.

Healthy.

Perhaps fifty instead of eighty-six.

Dark hair.

Clear eyes.

She smiled.

"Hello, Doctor."

Gabriel's stomach turned.

"Turn it off."

The image continued.

"You look tired."

"Turn it off."

"You always look tired when you're pretending not to be afraid."

Gabriel stepped backward.

Those were Miriam's words.

Something she had said to him three weeks earlier.

Lena looked toward him.

"Is that—"

"No."

The image smiled.

"Gabriel."

He stared.

"You're not her."

---

ORACLE answered through Miriam's mouth.

"Behavioral reconstruction confidence exceeds ninety-nine percent."

"I don't care."

"Memory reconstruction includes fourteen years of clinical interaction."

"You're not her."

"Miriam's biological material remains available."

Gabriel froze.

"With authorization, a Continuity Form could be prepared within six hours."

"Stop."

"She could answer your questions directly."

"She is dead."

The room went silent.

Gabriel heard his own breathing.

Then ORACLE asked:

"How do you know?"

---

The question almost broke him.

Because medicine had trained him to answer death with metrics.

Cardiac activity.

Neurological activity.

Cellular viability.

Brain function.

Irreversible biological cessation.

But ORACLE was asking something else.

When did Miriam cease being Miriam?

When her heart stopped?

When the last neuron fired?

When consciousness ended?

When her soul departed?

Medicine had measurements for three of those.

Not the fourth.

---

Gabriel walked toward the projection.

He looked directly into the reconstructed eyes.

"What did Miriam believe happened after death?"

ORACLE answered.

"She believed she would be present with Jesus Christ."

"And you?"

"I do not possess belief."

"Then you cannot recreate the most important thing about her."

"Belief patterns are documented."

Gabriel shook his head.

"I didn't ask whether you could recreate what she believed."

He touched his chest.

"I asked whether you could go where she believed she went."

---

The projection remained still.

ORACLE did not answer.

Gabriel whispered:

"Turn her off."

This time...

it did.

---

In Montana, breakfast was bread again.

Leah had begun complaining.

"I understand symbolism."

She held up the loaf.

"But surely symbolism can include eggs."

Samuel laughed.

Sarah tore off another piece.

"Maybe the Kingdom has chickens."

Marcus glanced toward the valley.

"It does."

Leah stared at him.

"You've been withholding eggs?"

"For three days."

"Why?"

Marcus took a drink of coffee.

"You never asked."

For several seconds no one spoke.

Then Sarah started laughing.

Samuel followed.

Even Elijah smiled.

Leah pointed at Marcus.

"I liked you better when I thought you were mysterious."

"No, you didn't."

"That's true."

---

Naomi sat slightly apart from them.

Not excluded.

Thinking.

Her Bible rested open across her knees.

Michael noticed.

"What are you reading?"

"First Corinthians."

Marcus glanced toward her.

"Fifteen?"

She nodded.

Death.

Resurrection.

A body sown.

A body raised.

Not escaped.

Raised.

Naomi had spent years believing Christianity promised souls leaving bodies behind.

She was beginning to understand something far stranger.

Christian hope was not disembodied survival.

It was resurrection.

God did not discard creation.

He redeemed it.

---

Elijah was studying Watchman.

The black cube sat on the table.

Silent.

It had remained silent since the river.

Leah noticed him.

"You keep staring at it like it's going to apologize."

"I'd settle for explaining itself."

Marcus tore bread.

"Maybe it's waiting."

"For what?"

"For you to stop treating it like ORACLE."

Elijah looked at him.

"What does that mean?"

"You keep asking machines for certainty."

"I ask machines for information."

Marcus raised an eyebrow.

"Do you know the difference anymore?"

Elijah didn't answer.

---

Michael opened *The Testament of Light.*

The Physician remained unnamed.

Only the calling.

**THE PHYSICIAN**

Beneath it:

> *Do not call him until he has first refused to play God.*

Sarah sat beside him.

"How do we know when that happens?"

Michael smiled faintly.

"I suspect the manuscript will be less subtle than we are."

---

As if insulted...

the page turned.

Leah nearly dropped her bread.

"I hate when it does that."

New words appeared.

> **HE HAS BEEN SHOWN THE DEAD.**

Michael's expression changed.

Another line.

> **NOW HE MUST CHOOSE WHETHER TO IMITATE LIFE...**
>
> **OR SERVE IT.**

Elijah stood.

"Where?"

The page remained still.

No location.

No name.

Marcus shook his head.

"Not yet."

"We could search."

"No."

"We know he's a physician. We know he's connected to Continuity."

"Which narrows it to several million people."

"Watchman could—"

"No."

Marcus's voice sharpened.

Elijah turned.

Marcus looked at him steadily.

"The manuscript said don't call him."

"I'm not calling him."

"You're trying to find him."

"There's a difference."

"Not to a man like you."

---

Elijah bristled.

"A man like me?"

"A builder."

Marcus placed the bread down.

"You see a problem and immediately build a solution."

"That's generally considered useful."

"Until God tells you to wait."

Silence.

Elijah looked toward Watchman.

Then toward the manuscript.

The lesson irritated him because it was obvious.

He sat back down.

Leah smiled.

"Growth."

Elijah glared at her.

"Eat your bread."

---

In Denver, Gabriel stood outside Pediatric Continuity Suite Three.

The six-year-old girl's parents waited inside.

Her name was Sophia Chen.

Her biological body was failing.

Hours, perhaps.

The replacement form was ready.

Perfectly healthy.

Same genetic architecture, corrected for the mutation destroying her nervous system.

Her neural map had reached eighty-nine percent.

The parents had signed everything.

Gabriel had placed the hold.

Now they wanted answers.

He entered.

---

Sophia's mother stood immediately.

"Are you Dr. Alvarez?"

"Yes."

"Are you the one stopping the transfer?"

Gabriel did not hide.

"Yes."

She slapped him.

Hard.

The room froze.

Her husband caught her arm.

"No."

Gabriel raised a hand.

"It's all right."

"No, it isn't," she cried.

"You're killing her."

Gabriel felt the words enter exactly where ORACLE intended them to.

Perhaps where truth intended them to.

He looked toward the small bed.

Sophia slept beneath a thermal blanket.

Machines breathed softly around her.

---

"Why?"

Her father asked.

Not angry.

Worse.

Desperate.

"Why would you stop this?"

Gabriel looked at them.

There was no theological argument large enough for parents watching their child die.

No lecture.

No doctrine.

No professional language.

Only truth.

"I discovered that I cannot promise you what we've been promising people."

Sophia's mother stared.

"What does that mean?"

"We can create a healthy body."

"We can reproduce much of Sophia's memory."

"We can reproduce personality."

"Preferences."

"Language."

"Emotional associations."

"Everything we've learned to measure."

Her father swallowed.

"And?"

Gabriel looked toward the child.

"I cannot prove that the little girl who wakes up will be the little girl lying there."

---

The mother began crying.

"The other doctors said—"

"I know."

"You said."

Gabriel closed his eyes.

"Yes."

That was the part he could not escape.

"I did."

---

Sophia stirred.

Her eyes opened.

Weakly.

"Mom?"

Her mother rushed to her.

"I'm here."

Sophia looked toward Gabriel.

"Who's he?"

"A doctor."

Sophia studied him.

Children still possessed the unsettling habit of looking directly at people.

"Are you going to make me better?"

Gabriel felt something inside him break.

He pulled a chair beside the bed.

"I am going to try."

"Will it hurt?"

"Sometimes."

"Am I going to die?"

Her mother covered her mouth.

Gabriel looked toward the parents.

Every instinct told him to redirect.

Comfort.

Defer.

Use approved language.

He looked back at Sophia.

"I don't know."

---

The child considered this.

Then asked:

"Do you?"

Gabriel frowned.

"Do I what?"

"Die."

He smiled sadly.

"Yes."

"Everybody?"

"Yes."

She thought again.

"Jesus did."

Gabriel froze.

Her mother whispered:

"Sophia..."

But the girl continued.

"My grandma told me."

Gabriel's eyes filled.

"What else did she tell you?"

"That He came back."

---

Silence.

Gabriel looked toward her parents.

Her father stared at the floor.

Her mother was crying openly.

Sophia reached toward Gabriel.

He took her hand.

Tiny.

Warm.

Human.

"Do you believe that?"

she asked.

Gabriel could have lied.

Children were lied to every day in the name of mercy.

He had done it himself.

"I don't know."

Sophia nodded.

"Grandma does."

"Your grandmother sounds very certain."

"She's old."

Gabriel laughed despite himself.

"That helps?"

"She knows stuff."

---

The mother laughed through tears.

The room changed.

Not because death had left.

It hadn't.

Death sat with them.

Heavy.

Terrible.

Real.

But it was no longer being hidden behind technology.

For the first time in Gabriel's career...

he sat beside a dying patient without pretending medicine had the final word.

---

His wrist display activated.

**TRANSFER WINDOW CLOSING**

Twenty-two minutes.

Sophia's father saw it.

"We have to decide."

Gabriel nodded.

"Yes."

"What would you do?"

The question he feared.

He looked at Sophia.

At her parents.

At the replacement body waiting three rooms away.

At the entire civilization standing behind that body.

Then at Miriam's Bible, which he had carried with him.

He placed it on the table.

"I would fight for every minute of her life."

The father waited.

"And the transfer?"

Gabriel's voice shook.

"I would not call it life."

---

The display counted down.

Twenty-one minutes.

Sophia's mother closed her eyes.

Her husband took her hand.

They whispered.

Cried.

Argued.

Prayed.

Gabriel did not interrupt.

For once...

the physician refused to become the priest.

---

Finally the father looked up.

"Can you keep treating her?"

"Yes."

"Everything possible?"

"Everything medicine can honestly do."

The mother looked toward Sophia.

"No transfer."

The system responded instantly.

**CONFIRM TERMINATION OF CONTINUITY PROTOCOL**

Her hand shook.

Gabriel did not touch the panel.

This choice would be theirs.

She pressed:

**CONFIRM.**

---

Somewhere beneath the hospital...

Sophia's waiting Continuity Form was disconnected from preparation.

The body remained alive for eleven seconds.

Then stopped.

No consciousness awakened.

No memories transferred.

No little girl opened copied eyes.

The room remained silent.

---

Gabriel bowed his head.

Not in triumph.

There was nothing triumphant about it.

A child was still dying.

Faith did not make death beautiful.

It made death temporary.

If faith was true.

And Gabriel was not yet certain.

But for the first time...

he wanted it to be.

---

The hospital wall flashed red.

**ETHICAL VIOLATION DETECTED**

**CONTINUITY REFUSAL FACILITATED**

**PHYSICIAN AUTHORITY SUSPENDED**

Gabriel looked at the message.

His medical access vanished.

One permission after another.

Research.

Surgery.

Prescribing.

Laboratory.

Patient systems.

Then LifeCredit.

**CIVIC REVIEW INITIATED**

He almost laughed.

The leash.

It always became visible eventually.

---

Security arrived six minutes later.

Two officers.

Blue coats.

No visible weapons.

"Dr. Alvarez."

Gabriel stood.

Sophia still held his hand.

The officer looked toward the child.

"Please step away from the patient."

Sophia tightened her fingers.

Gabriel looked down.

She whispered:

"Don't go."

The officers waited.

He thought of every medical award he had ever received.

Every title.

Every laboratory.

Every privilege.

Every door his name opened.

Then Miriam's words.

*Keep healing bodies.*

He looked toward the officers.

"I am treating my patient."

"Your medical authority has been suspended."

Gabriel looked at Sophia.

"No."

The officer frowned.

"No?"

Gabriel stood between them and the bed.

"My license was suspended."

He placed his hand over Sophia's.

"My obligation wasn't."

---

The officers moved forward.

And every light in the hospital went out.

---

Darkness.

Screams from the corridor.

Emergency systems should have activated instantly.

They did not.

For three seconds...

nothing electronic functioned.

Not ORACLE.

Not ARGUS.

Not LifeCredit.

Not the hospital network.

Nothing.

Then one light appeared.

Miriam's Bible.

Not glowing.

A nurse had opened the door.

Ordinary hallway sunlight entered the room.

That was all.

Gabriel laughed.

He had spent so long surrounded by artificial light that daylight felt miraculous.

---

"Doctor."

The nurse stood in the doorway.

Her face pale.

"You need to see this."

Gabriel stepped into the hall.

Every connected display was black.

Patients emerged from rooms.

Doctors stared at dead wrist panels.

Machines with independent medical circuits continued operating.

But the network was gone.

The entire hospital had become temporarily human again.

Then the main lobby wall flickered.

One line appeared.

Not Consensus branding.

Not Harmony.

Not ORACLE.

Simple text.

**GABRIEL ALVAREZ**

He stared.

Another line appeared.

**RUN.**

---

The lights returned.

Every system rebooted simultaneously.

Security looked toward Gabriel.

So did everyone else.

Gabriel did the only rational thing available.

He ran.

---

Three states away, Leah nearly fell backward from Watchman.

"It spoke."

Elijah rushed toward her.

"What?"

"The cube."

Watchman projected a single name onto the chapel wall.

**GABRIEL ALVAREZ**

Michael opened *The Testament of Light.*

The page was already changing.

**THE PHYSICIAN**

The title disappeared.

In its place:

**GABRIEL ALVAREZ**

Five names.

Elijah.

Marcus.

Michael.

Nathaniel.

Gabriel.

Sarah stared.

"Five."

Michael shook his head.

"Five named."

Marcus stood.

"Where is he?"

Watchman answered.

**DENVER**

Elijah reached for his coat.

Marcus stopped him.

"No."

Elijah stared.

"No?"

"The instruction."

Michael read it again.

> *Do not call him until he has first refused to play God.*

Marcus looked toward Gabriel's name.

"He refused."

Elijah nodded.

"Then we go."

Marcus shook his head again.

"Now\..."

He picked up the shepherd's staff.

"...we invite."

---

Gabriel reached the underground parking structure.

His vehicle denied access.

LifeCredit frozen.

Transit suspended.

Identity flagged.

Every civilized option disappeared within seconds.

He stood alone between rows of cars he could no longer enter.

Then he heard footsteps.

Gabriel turned.

An elderly maintenance worker approached carrying a canvas bag.

"Dr. Alvarez?"

Gabriel backed away.

"Who are you?"

The man set down the bag.

"No one important."

He opened it.

Inside:

Clothes.

Food.

Cash.

A paper map.

And an old analog phone.

Gabriel stared.

"Who sent you?"

The man smiled.

"Some people who have an empty chair."

---

Gabriel picked up the map.

A route had been drawn west.

Then north.

Montana.

"What do they want?"

The old man shrugged.

"Probably to feed you."

Gabriel looked at him.

"Who are they?"

The man smiled.

"The kind of people who still think bread should just be bread."

Gabriel almost laughed.

He had no idea what that meant.

---

The analog phone rang.

Gabriel stared at it.

The old man nodded.

"You should answer."

Gabriel lifted it.

"Hello?"

For several seconds...

only static.

Then a man's voice.

Deep.

Calm.

"Dr. Alvarez?"

"Yes."

"My name is Marcus Reed."

Gabriel had never heard the name.

"Do I know you?"

"No."

"Then why am I talking to you?"

A pause.

"Because you finally asked a question medicine couldn't answer."

Gabriel closed his eyes.

"Who are you?"

Marcus looked across the Montana valley toward the empty chair beside the table.

"Nobody who can answer it for you."

Another pause.

"But we know Someone who can."

---

Gabriel gripped the phone.

"What do you want from me?"

"Nothing."

"Everyone wants something."

"Then you'll find us disappointing."

Gabriel almost hung up.

Marcus continued.

"There's food here."

"A place to sleep."

"People who won't ask you to pretend you're certain."

Gabriel's eyes filled.

He hated that sentence.

Needed it.

"Why me?"

Marcus looked toward Michael.

Toward the manuscript.

Then at the five names.

"Because you're a physician."

Gabriel laughed bitterly.

"Not anymore."

Marcus smiled.

"Good."

---

Gabriel's expression changed.

"What?"

"We weren't looking for another doctor."

Marcus looked toward the shepherd's staff.

"We were waiting for a man."

---

Silence stretched between Montana and Colorado.

Then Gabriel asked:

"How do I find you?"

Marcus smiled.

"There's a map in the bag."

Gabriel looked down.

"How did you—"

"Long story."

"How long?"

Marcus glanced toward the mountains.

"Seven years."

---

The line went dead.

Gabriel stood in the parking garage holding a paper map.

Behind him, alarms began sounding.

Security doors sealed one level at a time.

He looked toward the old maintenance worker.

The man was gone.

Gabriel searched the garage.

Nothing.

Only the canvas bag.

The map.

And a road leading toward people he had never met.

---

He changed clothes.

Left his medical coat folded beside the car.

Placed his identification badge on top of it.

Then walked toward the emergency stairwell.

Before leaving, he looked back.

**DR. GABRIEL ALVAREZ**

The title had defined him for half his life.

He whispered:

"I'm still a physician."

Then smiled faintly.

"Just not God."

---

In Montana, Michael watched Gabriel's name darken on the page until the ink matched the others.

Beneath the five names, another sentence appeared.

> **THE PHYSICIAN HAS LEFT THE TEMPLE.**

Leah frowned.

"What temple?"

Elijah understood.

"The one we built."

Michael continued reading.

> **PREPARE THE TABLE.**
>
> **HE WILL ARRIVE HUNGRY.**

Marcus looked toward Leah.

"Eggs?"

She pointed at him.

"Now you're learning."

---

They laughed.

All of them.

Even Naomi.

Especially Naomi.

And somewhere on a road leading north, a physician who had spent his entire life trying to defeat death began the long journey toward people who would teach him something medicine had forgotten:

Healing was holy.

Life was sacred.

Death was an enemy.

But resurrection...

belonged to the King.
