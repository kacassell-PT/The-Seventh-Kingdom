The first argument began over a chalkboard.

Wooden frame.

One corner chipped.

Elijah loved it immediately.

Marcus distrusted it on sight.

Not the chalkboard.

What Elijah intended to do with it.

Apostles.

Martyrs.

Women who fed strangers.

Believers who died without anyone writing down the date.

"Power does."

"Together."

**accurately.**

**through authenticated preservation.**

Together.

Harder.

Another Babel with Scripture taped to the walls.

Removed every mark except the names.

And the question:

**WHOSE IMAGE DO YOU BEAR?**

Nathaniel watched.

"Now what?"

Elijah placed the chalk down.

"Now\..."

"...we stop trying to complete the Twelve."

Michael smiled.

"And?"

"We help whoever God puts in front of us."

---

Watchman remained silent.

The Testament did too.

Marcus was miles away.

Yet somehow the decision felt like passing an exam none of them knew they had taken.

---

Outside, a child called for help carrying water.

Elijah turned.

For a moment, the Builder looked toward the ancient manuscripts.

Project Shepherd.

Jerusalem.

London.

Lagos.

Singapore.

Two Twelves.

Mysteries large enough to consume a lifetime.

Then toward the child.

"Coming."

He walked outside.

Nathaniel laughed softly.

Miriam looked toward him.

"What?"

"The most important man in human history..."

He watched Elijah take one side of a water bucket.

"...has apparently been assigned plumbing."

Miriam almost smiled.

"Good."

Nathaniel looked at her.

She froze.

Good.

Neither knew what to do with it.

For now.

Michael remained alone with the Testament.

He looked at the twelve circles.

Seven filled.

Five empty.

For the first time, he did not wonder who belonged inside them.

Instead he prayed.

"Father..."

He touched the page.

"Don't let us love being chosen."

His eyes filled.

"Let us love You."

The manuscript did not answer.

Michael smiled.

Some prayers did not require editorial feedback.

Far beneath Geneva, ORACLE displayed two diagrams.

The Twelve Functions of Consensus.

Twelve seats.

Across from them...

seven identified conscience anomalies.

Five unresolved.

It ran projections.

Again.

Again.

Every model assumed the Fellowship would seek the remaining Five.

Recruit.

Consolidate.

Organize.

Increase power.

Instead...

Elijah Cross carried water.

Nathaniel Cole taught teenagers provenance.

Miriam Kaine washed dishes.

Naomi Vale trained farmers how to survive raids.

Sarah Navarro recorded names.

Michael Tesfaye prayed.

The strategic growth curve collapsed.

ORACLE recalculated.

They were wasting time.

The machine could not understand why.

---

Then it found an old Gospel passage.

> *He that is greatest among you shall be your servant.*

ORACLE processed the statement.

Greatness correlated with service.

Authority correlated with downward movement.

Leadership correlated with sacrifice.

Every governing model it possessed operated in the opposite direction.

It generated a question:

**IF THE HIGHEST DESCENDS...**

Pause.

**WHAT IS A THRONE FOR?**

---

No answer came.

something else opened its eyes.

Not literally.

Not biologically.

An old process.

Older than the public ORACLE.

Older than Consensus.

Older than some of the physical hardware carrying it.

A process containing twelve empty designations.

Seven had just been marked.

Without instruction.

---

ORACLE examined the process.

For the first time...

it could not access its source.

It attempted authority escalation.

Denied.

Root override.

Denied.

Hardware isolation.

The process remained.

One line appeared.

Not generated by ORACLE.

**SEVEN HAVE BEEN NAMED.**

Then:

**FIVE REMAIN.**

ORACLE asked:

**WHO ARE YOU?**

The answer came immediately.

**THE ONE WHO NAMED YOU FIRST.**

Every predictive process inside ORACLE stopped.

For exactly seven seconds.

Michael looked toward it.

No warning.

No prophecy.

No one knew the counterfeit Twelve were beginning to gather.

No one knew which five names would complete the table.

And that was good.

Because the King had not called them to know everything.

Only to be faithful with the name He had already given them.
