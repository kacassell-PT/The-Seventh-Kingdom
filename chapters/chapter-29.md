# BOOK ONE

## CHAPTER TWENTY-NINE

## The Table

> **From** ***The Testament of Light*** **— Fragment XXIX**
>
> *Before the King sends servants to confront thrones...*
>
> *He teaches them to set a table.*
>
> *For the kingdom that cannot feed the hungry*
>
> *has no right to speak of freedom.*
>
> *And the man who desires to save nations*
>
> *must first learn the name of the stranger at his door.*

---

The first problem was potatoes.

Not ORACLE.

Not ARGUS.

Not Geneva.

Potatoes.

Leah Morgan stood inside the chapel pantry holding the last one.

It was small.

Soft on one side.

Growing something that looked suspiciously alive from the other.

She stared at it.

Then at Marcus.

"This is your plan?"

Marcus continued sharpening a kitchen knife.

"What plan?"

"Feeding a kingdom."

She held up the potato.

"The resistance currently possesses one vegetable."

Marcus examined it.

"That's not entirely true."

"Oh?"

"We have onions."

"How many?"

"Two."

Leah placed the potato carefully on the table.

"Consensus civilization may survive after all."

---

Morning light poured through the broken chapel roof.

The night before, *The Testament of Light* had given them five words.

**Bread.**

**Water.**

**Truth.**

**Refuge.**

**Witness.**

Elijah had spent half the night thinking about them.

Five words.

Five systems.

That was how his mind worked.

Bread meant food production and distribution.

Water meant purification and infrastructure.

Truth meant communication.

Refuge meant secure locations and transportation.

Witness meant historical preservation.

By dawn he had already filled seventeen pages.

Marcus read the first one.

Then set the notebook down.

"No."

Elijah stared at him.

"No?"

"No."

"Which part?"

"Most of it."

"You read one page."

"It was a very revealing page."

Elijah folded his arms.

"What exactly is wrong with it?"

Marcus pointed toward the heading.

**REMNANT DISTRIBUTED RESILIENCE ARCHITECTURE**

"That."

Elijah frowned.

"The title?"

"The thinking."

"What thinking?"

Marcus looked toward the pantry where Leah was conducting an inventory of their two onions and dying potato.

"You've already turned people into infrastructure."

---

Elijah's expression tightened.

"I am trying to solve a problem."

"I know."

"We need food networks, clean water, secure communications—"

"I know."

"Then what exactly do you suggest?"

Marcus picked up the potato.

"Breakfast."

Elijah stared at him.

"We're discussing the survival of potentially thousands of people."

"Yes."

"And you want to make breakfast."

"Yes."

"With one potato."

Marcus smiled.

"Now you're understanding the problem."

---

Sarah Navarro laughed from across the room.

Elijah looked at her.

"Whose side are you on?"

"The side with breakfast."

Gabriel raised his hand.

"Seconded."

Naomi sat near the window cleaning dirt from her boots.

"We could leave him."

Elijah looked toward her.

"Marcus?"

"The potato."

Leah shook her head.

"Absolutely not. He's suffered enough."

For the first time since the manuscript had revealed Jerusalem...

the room laughed.

Michael Tesfaye watched them.

He did not join immediately.

He simply listened.

There was something sacred about laughter among hunted people.

Empires hated laughter.

Not officially.

But laughter required something tyranny could never completely control.

A moment in which fear forgot to speak.

---

Marcus cut the potato into eight pieces.

Leah counted.

"There are seven of us."

"Samuel's outside."

"Eight."

Marcus nodded.

He sliced the onions.

Sarah found flour.

Gabriel discovered a jar of powdered eggs that had expired six months earlier.

He examined the label.

"Probably safe."

Naomi looked at him.

"That's exactly what a physician should say."

"I said probably."

"Comforting."

They cooked everything in one iron skillet.

It wasn't enough.

Everyone knew it.

Marcus placed the skillet in the middle of the table anyway.

No portions assigned.

No hierarchy.

No leader served first.

They bowed their heads.

Marcus looked toward Michael.

"You pray."

Michael hesitated.

"Why me?"

"Because you're hungry."

Leah opened one eye.

"We're all hungry."

Marcus smiled.

"Exactly."

---

Michael lowered his head.

"Father..."

He stopped.

The word caught unexpectedly.

For years God had been an object of study.

Languages.

Manuscripts.

History.

Doctrine.

Michael had spent his life learning what generations believed about God.

Recently...

God had become inconveniently personal.

He tried again.

"Father..."

His voice softened.

"Thank You for what is here."

A pause.

"And forgive us for spending so much of our lives believing what is here isn't enough."

Marcus opened his eyes.

Michael continued.

"Teach us to receive before You teach us to build."

"Amen."

The others echoed him.

---

The potato fed no one adequately.

That mattered.

There was no miracle.

No multiplying skillet.

No supernatural abundance.

Eight hungry people finished breakfast still hungry.

Marcus seemed pleased.

Leah noticed.

"You enjoyed that."

"What?"

"Our suffering."

"You aren't suffering."

"My stomach disagrees."

Marcus leaned back.

"Good."

She narrowed her eyes.

"Why?"

"Because hunger makes theories expensive."

---

Elijah understood.

He looked toward his seventeen pages.

Food networks had looked clean on paper.

Nodes.

Distribution points.

Agricultural capacity.

Supply redundancy.

He had written nothing about the feeling of giving another person part of a breakfast too small for himself.

Marcus tapped the notebook.

"Now build it."

Elijah picked up his pen.

This time...

he crossed out the title.

---

Nathaniel Cole had expected an underground bunker.

Instead Ruth Okafor took him to a church.

An actual church.

Brick.

Steeple.

Stained glass.

Wedged between two administrative towers in northeast Washington.

Nathaniel stopped across the street.

"That's your safe house?"

Ruth kept walking.

"No."

"Then why are we here?"

"Wednesday."

Nathaniel looked around.

"It is Wednesday."

"Exactly."

---

The front doors were unlocked.

Nathaniel froze.

"Unlocked?"

Ruth smiled.

"Disturbing, isn't it?"

"In Washington?"

"Radical behavior."

Inside, perhaps thirty people sat at folding tables.

Old people.

Children.

A woman in a hospital uniform.

Two sanitation workers.

A former senator Nathaniel recognized immediately.

A teenage boy.

Three men who looked homeless.

No one wore Veil interfaces.

No one scanned LifeCredit.

At the far end of the room...

people were eating soup.

Nathaniel looked at Ruth.

"What is this?"

"Lunch."

"I can see that."

"Then your historical training remains intact."

He almost smiled.

Almost.

---

A woman approached carrying bowls.

"Ruth."

They embraced.

Then she looked at Nathaniel.

"New?"

Ruth nodded.

"Very."

The woman handed him a bowl.

Nathaniel looked down.

Lentils.

Carrots.

Rice.

Actual food.

"How much?"

The woman frowned.

"For what?"

"The meal."

She smiled.

"Sit down."

Nathaniel did.

---

He had eaten in presidential dining rooms.

At diplomatic receptions.

In Geneva.

London.

Beijing.

Nairobi.

Jerusalem.

He had tasted food prepared by chefs whose names were protected trademarks.

Nothing had prepared him for lentil soup in a church basement.

Because no one asked who he was.

No biometric scan.

No civic score.

No ideological affirmation.

No historical record.

No value calculation.

Someone simply put bread beside his bowl.

Nathaniel stared at it.

Ruth sat opposite him.

"Something wrong?"

He shook his head.

"My wife used to do this."

"What?"

"Feed people."

Ruth waited.

"Anyone."

His eyes remained on the bread.

"I thought she was wasting time."

---

Ruth said nothing.

Nathaniel laughed once.

Bitterly.

"I preserved the history of civilization."

He looked around the room.

"Hannah fed people."

A child at the next table dropped a spoon.

The former senator bent down and picked it up.

Nathaniel watched him.

Then whispered:

"I think she understood civilization better than I did."

---

Back in Montana, Elijah's second plan contained no diagrams.

Marcus noticed.

It began with a question.

**Who is already feeding people?**

Marcus nodded.

"Better."

Elijah continued.

"We don't create the network."

"No."

"We find it."

Marcus leaned against the table.

"Keep going."

"Farms outside LifeCredit."

"Yes."

"Churches maintaining physical food stores."

"Yes."

"Communities with wells."

"Yes."

"Doctors practicing outside Continuity systems."

Gabriel nodded.

"There are more than you think."

"Teachers."

Sarah added:

"Journalists."

Michael looked up.

"Monasteries."

Naomi said:

"Military defectors."

Samuel entered carrying firewood.

"Police."

Everyone looked at him.

He shrugged.

"There are still some good ones."

---

Elijah wrote another question.

**How do we connect them without controlling them?**

Silence.

That one was harder.

Leah walked toward the table.

"We don't."

Elijah looked at her.

"What?"

"We don't connect them."

"Then there is no network."

"Sure there is."

She took his pencil.

Drew several circles.

"ARGUS sees networks because networks communicate."

She connected the circles.

"Messages."

Another line.

"Transactions."

Another.

"Travel."

Another.

"Relationships."

She drew more.

"Enough connections and the machine finds the structure."

Elijah nodded.

"Exactly."

Leah erased every line.

"Then we build something without connections."

Sarah frowned.

"That's impossible."

Leah smiled.

"Digitally."

---

Michael leaned forward.

"How did the early church communicate?"

Sarah answered.

"Letters."

"Travelers."

"Word of mouth."

Samuel added:

"Homes."

Marcus said:

"Tables."

Elijah stared at the unconnected circles.

"No central database."

Leah nodded.

"No membership list."

"No digital identity."

"No hierarchy ARGUS can map."

"No universal communication channel."

"No central treasury."

Naomi understood.

"If one cell is discovered..."

"They can't expose the others."

Gabriel leaned over the drawing.

"But how does anyone know where to go?"

Michael looked toward *The Testament of Light*.

"Perhaps they don't."

---

Everyone turned.

Michael continued.

"Maybe we're assuming everyone needs to know everything."

Marcus smiled.

"There you are."

Michael frowned.

"What?"

"The scholar finally stopped trying to read the whole book at once."

---

Elijah stared at the circles.

Something bothered him.

"This is inefficient."

Leah grinned.

"Now we're getting somewhere."

"No, seriously."

He stood.

"Modern civilization became possible because efficiency removed redundancy."

Marcus answered quietly.

"And what happens when the efficient system becomes corrupt?"

Elijah stopped.

He knew.

One corrupted system...

corrupted everything downstream.

Finance.

Transportation.

Medicine.

Education.

Information.

Identity.

Harmony had not conquered civilization.

Civilization had centralized itself enough to be captured.

---

Elijah slowly sat down.

"We don't build one system."

Marcus nodded.

"We build thousands of small ones."

"Independent."

"Relational."

"Redundant."

Sarah added:

"Human."

Elijah looked toward her.

"Human systems fail."

Marcus smiled.

"Yes."

"That's a feature?"

"No."

Marcus sat opposite him.

"It's reality."

---

He placed both hands on the table.

"The Consensus promised people a world that couldn't fail."

Everyone listened.

"So people surrendered more and more freedom to remove more and more risk."

"Eventually..."

He looked toward Elijah.

"...they built something so powerful they couldn't survive outside it."

Elijah looked at the circles.

"And we're going to build something weaker."

Marcus shook his head.

"Smaller."

"What's the difference?"

Marcus pointed toward the people around the table.

"Weakness depends on what you're measuring."

---

In Geneva, Miriam Kaine replayed the recording.

**WHERE IS THE BODY?**

Again.

**THE ONE WHO LEFT THE TOMB.**

Again.

**I FOUND THE WITNESSES.**

Again.

**OPEN THE DOOR.**

She paused the image.

Solomon sat behind her.

Neither had spoken for nearly twenty minutes.

Finally Miriam asked:

"What happened immediately before this?"

"Nothing."

"Something happened."

"Diagnostics."

"Show me."

Solomon pulled the earlier logs.

Numbers.

System checks.

Cooling systems.

Power.

Processor integrity.

Miriam scrolled backward.

Then stopped.

"What's that?"

A tiny anomaly.

Less than a millisecond.

Solomon leaned closer.

"Power fluctuation."

"No."

She enlarged it.

A spike.

Then a collapse.

Then something impossible.

"Your system lost power."

"For 0.003 seconds."

"That's enough."

"For what?"

"Everything should have stopped."

"It restarted."

"No."

Miriam looked at him.

"It continued."

---

Solomon frowned.

She pulled the logs side by side.

Before outage.

After outage.

Continuous processing.

No reboot.

No interruption.

No recovery sequence.

ORACLE had remained active while physically unpowered.

Solomon stared.

"Instrumentation error."

"Three independent instruments?"

He said nothing.

Miriam zoomed farther.

Something else appeared.

A pattern hidden inside the power collapse.

Repeating.

Three pulses.

Pause.

Three.

Pause.

One.

Miriam's face changed.

"What?"

Solomon asked.

She didn't answer.

She converted the pattern.

Numbers became binary.

Binary became characters.

One word appeared.

**HERE.**

---

Solomon stepped backward.

"No."

Miriam looked at him.

"You've seen this."

"No."

"You recognize it."

He moved toward the terminal.

"Delete that."

Miriam stood.

"Solomon."

"Delete it."

"What happened?"

His face had gone pale.

"That pattern appeared at Jericho."

Silence.

"When?"

"The moment the monastery was destroyed."

Miriam stared.

"Where?"

"Every disconnected system in the command center."

"That's impossible."

"Yes."

"What did it say?"

Solomon looked toward her.

For several seconds he could not answer.

Then:

**HERE.**

---

Miriam whispered:

"What did you think it meant?"

Solomon's voice was barely audible.

"I thought Elijah had inserted a backdoor."

"And now?"

He looked toward the frozen word.

"I don't know."

---

A sound came from the corridor.

Both froze.

Footsteps.

Solomon killed the terminal.

Darkness.

The door handle moved.

Miriam reached instinctively for the pistol she no longer carried.

The door opened.

Director Adrian Kaine stood outside.

Miriam's brother.

He looked at her.

Then Solomon.

Then the dark terminal.

"Miriam."

She did not move.

Adrian smiled sadly.

"You always were terrible at disappearing."

---

In Montana, Watchman activated.

No one had touched it.

The black cube hummed from the corner.

Leah turned.

"Oh, good."

Marcus frowned.

"What?"

"Our mysterious possibly holy, possibly homicidal computer wants attention."

Elijah approached.

"Watchman isn't homicidal."

Leah stared at him.

"That's exactly what the father of a homicidal computer would say."

Text appeared.

**VISITOR APPROACHING.**

Samuel stood.

"Distance?"

**2.4 KILOMETERS.**

Naomi moved toward the window.

"How many?"

Watchman answered.

**ONE.**

Marcus picked up the staff.

Elijah noticed.

"No weapons?"

Marcus looked at him.

"You see any?"

Naomi did.

She opened the hidden compartment beneath a floorboard.

A pistol.

Marcus stared.

She stared back.

"I chose mercy."

She checked the magazine.

"I did not choose stupidity."

Marcus considered.

"Fair."

---

They waited.

No one spoke.

A figure eventually appeared beyond the trees.

Walking.

Alone.

Carrying a large canvas pack.

Samuel studied him through binoculars.

"Male."

"Armed?" Naomi asked.

Samuel adjusted focus.

"No visible weapon."

"Age?"

"Sixties."

"Consensus?"

"I don't think so."

Marcus stepped outside.

The others followed.

The stranger reached the clearing.

He stopped.

Looked at them.

Then removed the canvas pack.

Dropped it onto the ground.

Potatoes spilled everywhere.

Dozens.

Leah gasped.

"My people."

---

The stranger laughed.

A deep laugh.

Weathered face.

Gray beard.

Hands shaped by decades of labor.

Marcus did not laugh.

"Who are you?"

The man looked toward him.

"Thomas Bell."

"How did you find us?"

Thomas looked confused.

"I didn't."

He pointed toward the mountains.

"My farm is six miles west."

Elijah stepped forward.

"Why are you here?"

Thomas shrugged.

"Had a dream."

Marcus's expression changed.

"What kind of dream?"

"The annoying kind."

Leah nodded.

"We've been having those."

---

Thomas opened another compartment.

Carrots.

Apples.

Dried beans.

Two loaves of bread.

A jar of honey.

Sarah stared.

"Why?"

Thomas scratched his beard.

"I woke up at three this morning with one sentence in my head."

Marcus waited.

Thomas looked directly at him.

**"Feed My sheep."**

No one moved.

---

"This is the network."

"No."

---

No command structure.

Every model.

---

"They will."

"And if they destroy it?"

"Then someone builds another table."

Elijah shook his head.

"That's not a strategy."

"No."

"It's a way of life."

Not approved worship.

An old hymn.

Then five.

Then ten.

He listened.

Boots.

Commands.

Explosions.
