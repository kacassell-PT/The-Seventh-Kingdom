The first sign that the network was working was confusion.

Not theirs.

ORACLE's.

Leah discovered it at 5:42 in the morning.

She was sitting at the edge of the chapel with a cup of coffee that had gone cold because she had forgotten to drink it.

Watchman displayed a regional ARGUS map.

Ordinarily, Leah could identify the machine's thinking by watching what it watched.

Attention was a kind of confession.

Cameras tightened around probable targets.

Drones shifted toward suspicious movement.

Financial models brightened around unauthorized purchasing.

Communication analysis formed patterns around repeated contact.

ARGUS always revealed what it feared.

This morning...

it feared everything.

---

Leah leaned closer.

"That's weird."

Elijah was beside her within seconds.

"What?"

She expanded the map.

Thousands of tiny alerts.

Unauthorized meals.

Off-grid medical care.

Unregistered shelters.

Water sharing.

Private transportation.

Religious gatherings.

Neighborhood patrols.

Analog education groups.

People repairing generators.

Farmers distributing produce without LifeCredit.

Nurses treating administrative-death citizens.

Teachers holding classes in living rooms.

Families accepting strangers.

Elijah stared.

"How many?"

Leah smiled.

"Too many."

---

ARGUS had spent decades learning how to locate organizations.

Organizations had structure.

Leaders.

Money.

Communication.

Territory.

Membership.

The Remnant had almost none of those things.

Instead...

someone in Kansas baked bread for a family whose account had frozen.

A mechanic in Ontario repaired an ambulance without registering the parts.

A mosque outside Detroit opened its kitchen after local food access failed.

A pastor in Nairobi shared a well with a neighborhood that had reported him for theological deviation six months earlier.

A group of teenagers in Prague began printing paper maps showing elderly residents which stairwells still worked during power restrictions.

A doctor in Manila treated citizens listed as administratively deceased.

None of them knew Marcus Reed.

Most had never heard of Elijah Cross.

Almost none knew *The Testament of Light* existed.

Yet the behavior spread.

It could no longer tell.

---

At Redemption Ridge, dawn approached.

The first child woke.

Then another.

Someone started a fire.

Someone else carried water.

Bread began mixing in a wooden bowl.

The Body woke slowly.

Not efficiently.

Not simultaneously.

Not by command.

One person at a time.

And perhaps that was why no empire had ever learned how to stop it.
