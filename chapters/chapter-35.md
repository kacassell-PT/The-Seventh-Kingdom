# BOOK ONE

## CHAPTER THIRTY-FIVE

## The Archivist Comes

> **From** ***The Testament of Light*** **— Fragment XXXV**
>
> *The Archivist will arrive carrying what the empire tried to bury.*
>
> *He will believe the weight is history.*
>
> *It is not.*
>
> *History can be carried in books.*
>
> *The greater burden is deciding whether the people who wrote the lie*
>
> *may still be present when the truth is read aloud.*

---

Nathaniel Cole hated the truck.

Not philosophically.

Physically.

It smelled like diesel, wet canvas, old coffee, and something Ruth Okafor insisted was livestock feed but Nathaniel strongly suspected had once been livestock.

Every hole in the road traveled directly through the suspension and into his spine.

There were no climate controls.

No sound dampening.

No adaptive seating.

No navigation.

No Veil overlay turning Montana into something more civilized than Montana.

Only road.

Weather.

Dust.

And Ruth humming badly beside him.

Nathaniel looked toward her.

"Do you know the words?"

"Some."

"Then why do you keep changing them?"

"Creative interpretation."

"It's a hymn."

"God survived jazz."

Nathaniel looked out the window.

"I regret leaving Washington."

"No, you don't."

"No."

He adjusted the box resting between his feet.

"I regret leaving Washington with you."

Ruth smiled.

"Better."

---

The box contained six drives.

Not the Archive.

There was no longer one Archive.

That had taken Nathaniel three days to accept.

The records had been broken apart and scattered through communities Ruth called houses.

Not safe houses.

Not cells.

Houses.

Each held pieces.

Original evidence.

Cross-references.

Paper indexes.

Witness testimony.

No one location could expose the whole.

No one historian could rewrite it alone.

Nathaniel found the arrangement barbaric.

He was beginning to suspect barbarism had been unfairly maligned.

---

The drives at his feet were different.

Jericho.

Project Shepherd.

Harmony's founding documents.

Early LifeCredit coercion studies.

Continuity mortality reports.

And one collection Nathaniel had insisted on carrying himself.

**Administrative Deaths.**

Seventy-three thousand names.

People the Consensus had declared nonexistent.

Some physically dead.

Some imprisoned.

Some living beneath names history had been ordered to forget.

Ruth glanced toward the box.

"You've checked it six times."

"Five."

"Six."

Nathaniel counted mentally.

She was right.

He disliked that.

---

"You don't trust the drives?" Ruth asked.

"I don't trust roads."

"Roads have been reliable for thousands of years."

"So had empires, according to people living inside them."

Ruth laughed.

Then her expression softened.

"You nervous?"

Nathaniel looked toward her.

"No."

She waited.

He sighed.

"Yes."

"About the table?"

"About one chair."

---

Ruth knew.

Miriam Kaine.

They had not spoken the name for nearly an hour.

Nathaniel preferred it that way.

At first he had refused to travel.

The message had sat before him:

**MIRIAM KAINE IS WITH US.**

**JERICHO WAS A LIE.**

**SHE SIGNED IT.**

**SHE WILL TESTIFY.**

**THE CHAIR IS STILL OPEN.**

Nathaniel had wanted to burn the paper.

Instead he folded it.

He had read it so many times the crease had begun to tear.

It sat now inside Hannah's Bible.

He had not decided whether that made the message holier or merely harder to throw away.

---

"You're allowed to be angry," Ruth said.

Nathaniel looked at the mountains.

"I know."

"You're allowed to leave."

"I know."

"You're allowed not to forgive her today."

He turned.

"You people have a disturbing theology of low expectations."

Ruth smiled.

"No."

She looked back toward the road.

"We have a high theology of grace and a realistic theology of people."

---

Nathaniel said nothing.

Ruth continued.

"Forgiveness isn't pretending your nervous system got the memo."

"That's not in Scripture."

"Neither is your phrase 'archival provenance integrity,' but I've heard it twenty-seven times."

He almost smiled.

Then stopped himself.

He was approaching Miriam Kaine.

Laughter felt disloyal to the dead.

Ruth noticed that too.

She noticed far too much.

"Hannah laughed after bad things happened."

Nathaniel's face hardened.

"Don't use my wife to make a point."

Ruth nodded immediately.

"You're right."

No defense.

No explanation.

Nathaniel looked toward her.

"That's all?"

"Yes."

"You aren't going to clarify?"

"No."

He stared.

Ruth kept driving.

Nathaniel whispered:

"Infuriating."

"I've been told."

---

At Redemption Ridge, Miriam had been awake since four.

She had changed shirts three times.

Not because any looked better.

None did.

Thomas Bell's community had given her donated clothes.

Work trousers.

Flannel shirt.

Old jacket.

Nothing resembling the black tailored uniforms she had worn for decades.

Without those clothes, she sometimes caught her reflection and did not recognize herself.

Perhaps that was good.

Perhaps not.

Today, however, she wanted armor.

There was none.

---

Sarah found her stacking chairs.

"Those were already stacked."

Miriam looked at the chairs.

"They needed moving."

"Where?"

Miriam pointed to the opposite wall.

Sarah followed her finger.

"They were there yesterday."

Miriam exhaled.

"You're very observant."

"Occupational hazard."

---

Sarah sat on the table.

"Nathaniel's coming."

"I know."

"Marcus told you?"

"Everyone told me."

Sarah watched her.

"You're afraid."

"Yes."

"Good."

Miriam looked toward her.

Sarah did not soften the word.

"You should be."

"I know."

---

Sarah's notebook rested closed beside her.

Miriam noticed.

"No interview?"

"Not today."

"Why?"

"Because this isn't journalism."

"What is it?"

Sarah thought.

"Something I don't want to steal."

---

Miriam nodded.

Then asked:

"Does he know?"

"About Jericho?"

"All of it."

Sarah hesitated.

"Enough."

"That isn't all."

"No."

Miriam looked toward the chapel door.

"What if he asks something I can't remember?"

"Say you don't remember."

"What if I remember differently?"

"Say that."

"What if my testimony makes things worse?"

Sarah's expression sharpened.

"For whom?"

Miriam stopped.

There it was.

The old instinct.

Manage consequences.

Control exposure.

Shape narrative.

Sarah saw it.

"So that's still in you."

Miriam looked toward her.

"Yes."

"Good."

Miriam almost laughed.

"Everyone here says that when I admit something terrible."

Sarah smiled faintly.

"Because hidden things grow teeth."

---

Marcus entered carrying cedar planks.

He looked at the chairs.

Then Miriam.

Then Sarah.

"Why are the chairs over there?"

Miriam closed her eyes.

Sarah answered:

"Security operation."

Marcus nodded.

"Makes sense."

He placed the cedar down.

Miriam looked at him.

"What are you building?"

"A longer table."

Her face changed.

"Because of Nathaniel?"

"Because there are more people."

Marcus measured the plank.

"Tables should adjust."

---

Miriam watched.

"Do you do this intentionally?"

"What?"

"Everything becomes a lesson."

Marcus looked genuinely puzzled.

"I'm making a table."

Sarah laughed.

Miriam did too.

The laughter escaped before she could stop it.

Then she felt guilty.

Marcus noticed.

He continued measuring.

"You're allowed."

She looked toward him.

"Allowed what?"

"To laugh before Nathaniel gets here."

Miriam's eyes filled.

She looked away.

Marcus said nothing else.

---

Naomi watched from outside.

She had not spoken to Miriam much.

Not since the confession.

They had shared intelligence rooms.

Operations.

Briefings.

Authority.

Now they shared food and avoided one another.

Naomi was not certain whether that counted as progress.

She approached.

Miriam saw her and stiffened.

Naomi noticed.

Good.

"Walk with me."

Miriam looked toward Sarah.

Sarah shrugged.

"She probably won't kill you."

Naomi looked at her.

"Helpful."

---

They walked toward the river.

Neither spoke until the chapel disappeared behind the trees.

Then Naomi stopped.

"Did you know?"

Miriam knew immediately.

"About Jericho?"

"About my team."

Miriam closed her eyes.

Ghost Division.

The protocol change.

The attempt to erase the witnesses.

"I knew the contingency existed."

"That's not what I asked."

"No."

Miriam looked at her.

"I did not know ORACLE activated it."

Naomi stared.

"Would you have?"

"What?"

"Activated it."

Miriam tried to answer quickly.

Naomi raised a hand.

"Don't."

She waited.

Miriam thought.

Not about who she was now.

Who she had been.

That woman mattered.

Finally:

"Five years earlier?"

"Yes."

"Maybe."

Naomi's face tightened.

Miriam continued.

"Ten?"

Her voice became smaller.

"Probably."

Naomi looked toward the river.

"Thank you."

Miriam blinked.

"For that?"

"For telling the truth before you knew whether I'd like the answer."

---

They stood quietly.

Then Naomi asked:

"Did you know about Daniel?"

Miriam's face changed.

No.

That wound was different.

"Yes."

Naomi stopped breathing.

Miriam continued before fear silenced her.

"Not the simulation details."

"But the Continuity recruitment list."

"Your son's case was escalated because you were a high-value recovery officer."

Naomi turned away.

Her shoulders shook once.

Miriam could not tell whether from anger or grief.

Probably both.

"We used grief to retain you."

Naomi said nothing.

"We knew officers who accepted Continuity for family members were less likely to defect."

Naomi finally looked back.

"You turned my son into a loyalty program."

Miriam began crying.

"Yes."

---

Naomi struck her.

One slap.

Hard.

Miriam stumbled.

Naomi's hand shook.

For one terrible second she wanted more.

Years.

Daniel.

The simulation.

The lie.

Everything.

Miriam stood still.

Did not defend herself.

Did not apologize.

Not yet.

Naomi raised her hand again.

Stopped.

Marcus's words returned.

Jesus didn't save me so I could become better at choosing who deserves mercy.

She lowered it.

"I don't forgive you."

Miriam's cheek reddened.

"I know."

"I don't even like you."

"I know."

Naomi laughed once through tears.

"That's probably going to take longer."

Miriam almost smiled.

"Understandable."

---

Naomi stepped closer.

"If you ever use Daniel to manipulate me again—"

"I won't."

"I'm not finished."

Miriam nodded.

"If you ever use anyone's dead child to manipulate anyone..."

Her voice broke.

"...I will forget every spiritual lesson Marcus has ever taught me for approximately thirty seconds."

Miriam nodded.

"Understood."

Naomi wiped her face.

Then looked at Miriam's cheek.

"Are you okay?"

Miriam laughed.

The absurdity overwhelmed both of them.

Soon Naomi was laughing too.

Not reconciliation.

Not yet.

But perhaps two women could stand beside a river after one struck the other and still choose not to become enemies.

That was something.

---

Watchman alerted at 2:43 p.m.

**VEHICLE APPROACHING.**

Leah looked up.

"Finally."

Elijah joined her.

"Identification?"

**TWO OCCUPANTS.**

Then:

**RUTH OKAFOR.**

Elijah frowned.

"Know her?"

Sarah shook her head.

Miriam did.

"Historical Harmonization."

Everyone looked toward her.

"Inside?"

"Very."

"Trusted?"

Miriam almost laughed.

"Apparently not by the Consensus."

---

Then the second name appeared.

**NATHANIEL COLE.**

The chapel changed.

Miriam went still.

Marcus looked toward her.

"You don't have to be in the room when he arrives."

"Yes."

He waited.

Miriam corrected herself.

"I do."

"No."

Marcus walked closer.

"You have to tell the truth."

"You do not have to stage your own execution."

She looked toward him.

"I signed Jericho."

"I know."

"He deserves to see me."

"Yes."

"Then—"

Marcus interrupted.

"He deserves your testimony."

His voice softened.

"Not your self-destruction."

---

Miriam looked down.

Another old instinct exposed.

Punishment pretending to be repentance.

"If he hates me?"

Marcus nodded.

"He might."

"If he tells me to leave?"

Marcus looked toward the table.

"He doesn't own the chairs either."

---

The truck appeared between the trees.

Nathaniel saw the chapel first.

Stone.

Broken roof.

Smoke from the chimney.

People.

Children.

Clothes drying outside.

He frowned.

"This is it?"

Ruth slowed.

"What were you expecting?"

"I don't know."

"A fortress?"

"Perhaps."

"A command center?"

"Maybe."

"A secret underground temple?"

Nathaniel looked at her.

"Now you're mocking me."

"Only because you make it rewarding."

---

The truck stopped.

Marcus approached.

No weapon.

Shepherd's staff.

Nathaniel knew his face from Project Shepherd.

Marcus Reed.

Operation Jericho.

The man whose monastery Miriam Kaine had helped destroy.

That fact came first.

History always came first for Nathaniel.

Marcus opened the passenger door.

"Nathaniel?"

"Yes."

"Marcus."

"I know."

Marcus smiled.

"That's going to happen a lot around here."

---

Nathaniel stepped down.

His knees nearly failed after hours in the truck.

Marcus pretended not to notice.

Nathaniel appreciated him immediately for that.

Ruth climbed out.

"Food?"

Marcus pointed toward the chapel.

"Always."

Ruth looked pleased.

Nathaniel looked toward the door.

"Is she inside?"

Marcus knew who.

"Yes."

Nathaniel's jaw tightened.

"Good."

Marcus studied him.

"Good?"

"No."

Nathaniel took the box from the truck.

"But if I traveled twelve hundred miles to avoid her, Hannah would haunt me."

Ruth smiled.

"She'd correct your footnotes."

Nathaniel looked toward her.

"That is haunting."

---

They walked toward the chapel.

Sarah met Ruth first.

Their eyes lingered.

Journalist.

Historian.

Both understood what the other carried.

Samuel shook Nathaniel's hand.

Leah introduced herself and immediately asked what encryption system protected the Archive drives.

Nathaniel replied that if she touched them without permission he would break her fingers.

Leah smiled.

"I like him."

Gabriel offered water.

Michael stared.

Nathaniel stared back.

"The Testament."

Michael nodded.

"The Archivist."

Nathaniel raised an eyebrow.

"Apparently we're using titles."

Michael smiled.

"Only until they become embarrassing."

---

Then Nathaniel saw Miriam.

Everything else disappeared.

She stood beside the table.

No uniform.

No insignia.

Bruise on one cheek.

Hands folded.

Afraid.

That surprised him.

Nathaniel had seen Miriam Kaine address international councils.

Cross-examine presidents.

Defend Harmony revisions before the Architects.

She had seemed incapable of fear.

Now she looked like a woman waiting for a verdict.

---

Nathaniel set the box down.

Miriam spoke.

"Nathaniel."

"Do not use my first name."

The words cut cleanly.

Miriam nodded.

"Mr. Cole."

"No."

She waited.

He looked toward her.

"Don't call me anything."

---

Marcus stepped backward.

So did everyone else.

Not leaving.

Making space.

Ruth moved toward the far wall.

Sarah closed her notebook.

Miriam stood alone.

Nathaniel approached.

He stopped three feet away.

"Did you sign the Jericho historical revision?"

"Yes."

"Did you know the site was a monastery?"

"Yes."

"Did you know children were present?"

"Yes."

"Did you know there were no verified chemical weapons?"

"Yes."

"Did you approve language saying there were?"

Miriam's voice shook.

"Yes."

---

Nathaniel's face flushed.

"Why?"

"We believed public knowledge of the operation would produce wider violence."

"Who is we?"

"The security council."

"Names."

Miriam hesitated.

Nathaniel's eyes sharpened.

"Names."

She gave them.

Seven.

Two dead.

Three still inside Consensus leadership.

One missing.

Solomon Vey.

Nathaniel listened.

Memorized.

---

"Who wrote 'armed religious extremists'?"

"I approved the phrase."

"Who wrote it?"

Miriam looked toward Sarah.

Then back.

"Dr. Elias March."

Nathaniel knew the name.

Chief historical semantics officer.

A man who later wrote the textbook Nathaniel had taught from.

His stomach turned.

"Who removed the casualty images?"

"I did."

Marcus looked down.

Nathaniel continued.

"Who removed Brother Matthias's name?"

Miriam's lips trembled.

"I did."

Michael closed his eyes.

"Why?"

"Named victims produce stronger public identification than aggregate casualties."

Nathaniel stared.

"You erased him because people might care."

Miriam began to cry.

"Yes."

---

Nathaniel struck the table.

Not her.

The sound startled everyone.

"You knew."

"Yes."

"You knew exactly what history does."

"Yes."

"And you used it."

"Yes."

"You understood memory better than half the historians in my department..."

His voice fractured.

"...and you turned forgetting into policy."

Miriam whispered:

"Yes."

---

Nathaniel turned away.

He wanted her to defend herself.

That would be easier.

He wanted euphemism.

Justification.

A sentence he could attack.

She gave him none.

Only yes.

Truth was depriving him of argument.

It left only grief.

---

He walked toward the broken chapel wall.

Hannah's Bible pressed against his chest beneath his coat.

He pulled it out.

Not intentionally.

His hand simply needed something.

The folded message fell from between the pages.

**THE CHAIR IS STILL OPEN.**

It landed on the floor.

Miriam saw it.

Nathaniel did too.

Neither moved.

---

Marcus bent down.

Picked it up.

Handed it to Nathaniel.

No commentary.

Nathaniel hated him slightly for that.

---

Miriam whispered:

"There's more."

Nathaniel turned sharply.

"Of course there is."

She flinched.

Good.

He wanted something to hurt.

Then hated himself for wanting it.

"What?"

Miriam looked toward the box he carried.

"Administrative deaths."

Nathaniel went still.

"How do you know?"

"I helped authorize the framework."

Ruth's expression changed.

Even she had not known that.

Miriam continued.

"It began as identity suppression for undercover security operations."

"Then?"

"Dissidents."

"Then?"

"Religious networks."

Her eyes moved toward Ruth.

"Then communities we couldn't dismantle conventionally."

---

Nathaniel looked at Ruth.

Miriam followed.

"Ruth Freeman?"

Ruth nodded.

Miriam swallowed.

"I signed yours."

Ruth said nothing.

Nathaniel stared.

One woman.

Two people at the same table whose lives Miriam had erased differently.

A monastery.

A human being.

Memory.

Identity.

---

Ruth approached slowly.

Miriam looked toward her.

"I am sorry."

Ruth's expression remained calm.

"You don't know enough yet."

Miriam blinked.

"About what?"

"What happened after you erased me."

Ruth pulled out a chair.

Sat.

"You thought deletion ended something."

Miriam said nothing.

Ruth smiled faintly.

"It began my ministry."

---

Leah laughed softly.

Miriam looked confused.

Ruth continued.

"Once I was legally dead, LifeCredit couldn't threaten me."

"No employment to lose."

"No travel permission to revoke."

"No account to freeze."

"No reputation score."

Her smile widened.

"You accidentally made me administratively free."

---

Even Nathaniel smiled.

Only briefly.

Miriam began to laugh.

Then cry.

Ruth reached across the table.

Not taking Miriam's hand.

Placing bread between them.

"Don't romanticize it."

Her voice hardened.

"My husband lost his pension."

"My daughter couldn't claim me as family."

"I missed my mother's funeral because cameras would have identified me."

"People suffered because of what you signed."

Miriam's laughter vanished.

Ruth continued.

"But you do not get to decide that the worst thing you did is the only thing God was allowed to do afterward."

---

Miriam stared.

Ruth tore the bread.

Half.

Placed one piece on Miriam's side.

"Eat."

---

Nathaniel looked away.

Too much grace irritated him.

Perhaps because it revealed how much anger he still carried.

He found Marcus outside.

Of course.

The man seemed to know when rooms needed fewer people.

Nathaniel stood beside him.

Neither spoke.

Finally:

"You're Marcus Reed."

"Yes."

"Jericho."

Marcus looked toward the valley.

"Yes."

Nathaniel waited for something.

Nothing came.

"She helped kill your people."

"Yes."

"Yes."

"How?"

"Poorly."

---

Nathaniel blinked.

Marcus continued.

"I haven't forgiven everything."

"You haven't?"

"No."

Nathaniel stared.

Some part of him had expected the Shepherd to dispense instant holiness like medicine.

Marcus seemed almost amused.

"Disappointed?"

"A little."

"Good."

---

Nathaniel looked toward the river.

"I wanted you to tell me how to forgive her."

"I can't."

"Then what are you useful for?"

Marcus smiled.

"Mostly fences."

Nathaniel laughed despite himself.

Then covered his face.

"I hate all of you."

"No, you don't."

Marcus nodded.

"We have limited material."

---

Nathaniel's laughter turned into tears.

He sat on a stone.

Marcus waited.

Finally Nathaniel whispered:

"Hannah was one of them."

"Who?"

"The people who remembered."

Marcus sat beside him.

"She knew what was happening before I did."

Nathaniel stared at his hands.

"I was the historian."

"Hmm."

"Don't."

Marcus nodded.

Nathaniel continued.

"I thought I was protecting truth."

"I was protecting access."

"Credentials."

"Systems."

"My place inside the institution."

"Hannah fed people."

"Copied records."

---

Turned it in his hand.

"With technology?"

"Yes."

"Yes."

"Naomi with security."

"Yes."

Nathaniel looked toward him.

"And you?"

"Violence."

Each of them.

A good thing.

Received as vocation.

Slowly converted into authority.

Then identity.

Then idol.

---

Nathaniel looked toward the chapel again.

"What did Miriam mistake?"

Marcus followed his eyes.

"Order."

---
