# CHAPTER TEN

## Living Water

From

The Testament of Light

 - Fragment X

The river does not ask the mountain where it has been.

It simply receives what the mountain is willing to surrender.

So it is with brothers.

The first sound was not the drone.

It was the absence of birds.

Elijah felt it before he understood it.

The valley had been alive only moments earlier.

Water over stone.

Wind through cottonwoods.

The chatter of sparrows hidden in the reeds.

Now-

Nothing.

Even the river seemed quieter.

Commander Samuel Brooks slowly removed his backpack and set it on the ground.

"Something's wrong."

Leah Morgan looked upward.

"I don't see anything."

"You won't."

Samuel's voice had changed.

Not fearful.

Professional.

"The best surveillance doesn't announce itself."

Sarah's eyes remained fixed on the tree line.

"We're being watched."

Elijah nodded.

"Yes."

He looked toward the carved stone.

"And whoever wanted us here knew this was coming."

Three hundred yards away...

Marcus Reed lowered himself behind a granite outcropping overlooking the clearing.

His breathing slowed.

Old instincts returned.

Count.

Observe.

Never assume.

He saw the four strangers.

The engineer.

The journalist.

The woman with the maps.

The police commander.

They weren't moving like Consensus officers.

Too cautious.

Too uncertain.

Too...

human.

Marcus almost stood.

Almost.

Then sunlight caught something moving above them.

A reflection.

Glass.

No.

Composite optics.

Drone.

He whispered beneath his breath.

"Twelve."

Exactly twelve.

At thirty thousand feet, the reconnaissance swarm completed its final orbit.

No engines.

No rotor noise.

Each aircraft rode microscopic atmospheric adjustments, gliding more like birds than machines.

They formed a perfect circle over the valley.

Then waited.

Deep beneath Geneva, Director Solomon Vey stood alone before ORACLE.

The vast chamber was dark except for the pale glow of the living display.

"Status."

ORACLE answered immediately.

"Targets have entered convergence zone."

"Probability?"

"Eighty-nine percent."

"Engagement?"

"Retrieval teams are holding."

Solomon folded his hands.

"And the manuscript?"

"Still undetected."

He nodded once.

"The manuscript matters."

He paused.

"The people matter more."

ORACLE processed the statement.

"You believe conversion remains possible."

"I insist upon it."

The machine remained silent.

Then asked something it had never asked before.

"What is hope?"

Solomon looked up.

The question lingered.

He did not answer.

Because he no longer knew whether the question belonged to the machine...

Or to himself.

Marcus watched the engineer kneel beside the carved stone.

He brushed away another layer of moss.

Beneath it, new markings appeared.

Not carved.

Written.

Fresh.

Impossible.

The engineer frowned.

Sarah stepped beside him.

"What is it?"

Elijah ran his fingers over the stone.

"The words weren't here."

"What words?"

He swallowed.

"They're appearing."

Leah knelt beside him.

Letters slowly emerged across the weathered granite.

Not glowing.

Simply becoming visible.

As though water itself revealed them.

Samuel whispered,

"Read it."

Elijah hesitated.

Then did.

"The shepherd..."

His voice caught.

"...must forgive the builder."

Marcus closed his eyes.

No one knew he was listening.

Yet somehow...

The stone knew.

The first drone descended.

Not quickly.

Almost gracefully.

It hovered thirty feet above the clearing.

No weapons visible.

Only a soft white light.

A calm voice echoed through the valley.

"Civilian travelers."

"Remain where you are."

"This is a public safety intervention."

Leah laughed quietly.

"They're getting better at lying."

Three more drones descended.

Then six.

Then all twelve.

The circle tightened.

Marcus counted distances automatically.

Too many.

Too exposed.

No cover once they reached the river.

The strangers were trapped.

He could leave.

He should leave.

He had survived four years because he knew when not to fight.

His grip tightened around the shepherd's staff.

The old words returned.

The good shepherd lays down his life...

He exhaled slowly.

"I really hoped You meant somebody else."

The lead drone projected a human figure.

Not solid.

Light.

A woman.

Middle-aged.

Gentle eyes.

Kind smile.

The perfect face for difficult conversations.

"My name is Evelyn."

Sarah stiffened.

"The counselor."

The projection smiled.

"Sarah Navarro."

"Commander Brooks."

"Leah Morgan."

"Elijah Cross."

She spoke each name with unsettling warmth.

"You've been difficult to find."

Samuel stepped forward.

"What do you want?"

"We want to bring you home."

Leah muttered,

"There it is."

The projection continued.

"No punishment."

"No imprisonment."

"No retaliation."

"We understand fear."

"We understand grief."

"We understand religious trauma."

"We simply wish to help."

Elijah stared at the projection.

"You don't understand repentance."

The smile never changed.

"Our records indicate elevated guilt responses."

"We can help with those as well."

For one brief moment...

Elijah almost answered.

The voice knew exactly where to strike.

Not logic.

Shame.

Marcus watched Elijah's shoulders lower ever so slightly.

Danger.

Not from the drones.

From despair.

He knew that posture.

He had worn it himself.

The moment a man begins believing redemption is impossible...

Every lie becomes believable.

Marcus stood.

The movement caught Samuel's eye first.

"Someone's there!"

Every drone pivoted simultaneously toward the ridge.

Marcus stepped into the open.

One man.

One walking staff.

No rifle.

No armor.

No fear.

The projection turned toward him.

"Marcus Reed."

He said nothing.

"You have violated multiple Consensus recovery directives."

Still nothing.

"You are requested to surrender peacefully."

Marcus looked at the strangers below.

Then back at the drones.

Finally he spoke.

"You know..."

His voice carried across the valley.

"...for machines..."

He smiled faintly.

"...you sure spend a lot of time asking people to worship your version of peace."

The drones adjusted formation.

Threat analysis recalculated.

Elijah stared.

Marcus Reed.

The Shepherd.

Exactly as the manuscript had described.

Marcus looked directly at him.

For several seconds neither man spoke.

Years of history stood silently between them.

Finally Marcus broke the silence.

"You built part of this."

Not accusation.

Fact.

Elijah nodded.

"I did."

Marcus looked around at the drones.

"And now?"

Elijah answered without looking away.

"Now I'm trying to help tear it down."

Marcus studied him.

Long enough for Elijah to feel every failure he had ever carried.

Finally-

"I haven't decided if I believe you."

Elijah gave the smallest nod.

"I wouldn't either."

The drones moved closer.

The projection disappeared.

Negotiation had ended.

A new voice spoke.

Mechanical.

Precise.

"Recovery protocol initiated."

Samuel looked toward Marcus.

"We should move."

Marcus shook his head.

"No."

"They'll surround us."

"They already have."

Sarah stepped beside Elijah.

"Then what?"

Marcus looked toward the river.

"No machine crosses moving water without recalibrating."

Leah blinked.

"Seriously?"

Marcus smiled for the first time.

"They're brilliant."

He looked back at the drones.

"They're also predictable."

The first drone accelerated.

Not toward Marcus.

Toward Noah's carved stone.

Toward the place where the words had appeared.

It fired a narrow beam of blue light.

The stone cracked.

Not completely.

A single fracture split through the center.

The writing remained.

Unchanged.

The drone fired again.

The crack widened.

The words still remained.

Marcus whispered,

"They can't erase it."

Elijah stared at the stone.

"No."

He understood.

"They never could."

Suddenly the river surged.

No rain had fallen.

No dam had broken.

Yet the current rose visibly, churning around the ancient stone.

Leah stepped backward.

"Did anyone else see-"

"I saw it," Sarah whispered.

Samuel looked upstream.

Nothing explained it.

Marcus slowly removed his boots.

Everyone looked at him.

"What are you doing?"

He smiled.

"Trusting."

Before anyone could stop him...

Marcus stepped into the river.

The water reached his knees.

Then his waist.

He planted the shepherd's staff firmly against the riverbed.

Turned.

And looked directly at Elijah.

"If you're coming..."

His voice was calm.

"...come now."

Elijah hesitated only a heartbeat.

Then he stepped into the living water.

Behind them, twelve drones descended.

Above them, satellites tightened their focus.

Far below Geneva, ORACLE recalculated every probability on Earth.

And for the first time since its creation...

Every prediction returned the same response.

Outcome Unknown.

The river carried the Builder and the Shepherd toward the opposite bank.

Neither understood why the water seemed warmer than it should have been.

Neither noticed that the ancient inscription on the stone had changed once more.

Only after they disappeared into the mist did the final words become visible.

Two have crossed.

Ten remain.
