# BOOK ONE

## CHAPTER TWENTY-EIGHT

## Stay

> **From** ***The Testament of Light*** **— Fragment XXVIII**
>
> *The first command given to the gathered was not Go.*
>
> *It was Stay.*
>
> *For a man who cannot remain with his brother when escape is possible*
>
> *will abandon him when sacrifice is required.*
>
> *Before the Kingdom sends a people into the world...*
>
> *it teaches them not to leave one another.*

---

Marcus Reed woke to the sound of an argument.

Not gunfire.

Not drones.

Not alarms.

An argument.

He smiled.

For reasons he could not explain, that felt like progress.

---

The voices came from the chapel.

Leah was loudest.

"No."

Samuel answered with considerably less volume.

"That's not an argument."

"It is absolutely an argument."

"It's a word."

"It's an important word."

Marcus entered carrying an armful of firewood.

Everyone was already awake.

Elijah sat at the table.

Sarah leaned against the wall drinking coffee.

Michael had *The Testament of Light* open beside him.

Naomi stood near the doorway.

Gabriel looked as though he had immediately regretted joining whatever conversation had begun before breakfast.

Marcus dropped the wood beside the stove.

"What word?"

Leah pointed toward the manuscript.

"Stay."

Marcus looked at Michael.

Michael nodded.

"The passage hasn't changed."

He turned the book so Marcus could read it.

> **TOMORROW, TEACH THEM TO STAY.**

Marcus stared at the sentence.

Leah folded her arms.

"So?"

"So what?"

"What are you teaching us?"

Marcus looked at her.

"Nothing."

Leah blinked.

"Nothing?"

"Correct."

"The supernatural manuscript gave you an assignment."

"Apparently."

"And you're ignoring it?"

"No."

"Then what are we doing?"

Marcus looked toward the broken fence outside.

"Work."

---

Leah stared.

"Work."

"Yes."

"That's your spiritual insight?"

Marcus picked up his gloves.

"The north fence still needs repairing."

He pointed toward Gabriel.

"Spring needs clearing."

Toward Samuel.

"Roof leaks over the back room."

Toward Sarah.

"We need bread."

Sarah raised her cup.

"I didn't realize journalism prepared me for baking."

"It doesn't."

Then Elijah.

"Woodpile."

Elijah frowned.

"Woodpile?"

"Move it."

"Where?"

Marcus pointed across the clearing.

"There."

Elijah looked.

The woodpile stood perhaps twenty feet from where Marcus wanted it.

"Why?"

Marcus shrugged.

"It needs moving."

Leah laughed.

"Oh, this is definitely theological."

---

Naomi had not moved.

Marcus looked at her.

"You come with me."

"Where?"

"Fence."

"I know nothing about fences."

"Good."

Marcus walked outside.

Naomi stared after him.

Gabriel smiled.

"I think that's the point."

She glared.

"Your eggs were worse."

"I didn't cook them."

"Exactly."

Leah pointed toward her.

"Careful."

---

For the next four hours...

nothing important happened.

Which made it one of the most important mornings they had spent together.

---

Elijah moved wood.

One piece at a time.

At first he tried to optimize the task.

Stacking according to length.

Calculating carrying capacity.

Finding the shortest route.

Marcus walked past once.

"You know you can just carry it."

Elijah looked at him.

"I am carrying it."

"No."

Marcus continued walking.

"You're engineering it."

Elijah stared after him.

Then at the wood.

He picked up three logs.

Carried them twenty feet.

Dropped them.

Returned.

There was something deeply offensive about inefficient labor.

He suspected Marcus knew that.

---

Sarah baked bread.

Badly.

The first loaf collapsed.

The second burned.

The third somehow managed both.

Michael entered as she removed it.

He examined the loaf.

"What happened?"

"Persecution."

"By whom?"

"The oven."

Michael nodded solemnly.

"I have read of such things."

Sarah threw a towel at him.

---

Gabriel cleared debris from the spring.

Leaves.

Branches.

Mud.

Nothing intellectually demanding.

His hands became numb in the water.

He found himself thinking about the Continuity laboratories.

Everything there had been sterile.

White.

Precise.

Human bodies suspended in perfect artificial environments.

No dirt.

No weather.

No decay.

No unpredictability.

Here he pulled rotting leaves from water people would drink.

Life touching death.

Decay feeding soil.

Nothing wasted.

He wondered whether creation had always been preaching and men had simply become too intelligent to listen.

---

Samuel repaired the roof.

Leah was supposed to help.

She did not.

Instead she sat beside him arguing that structural maintenance was outside her skill set.

Samuel handed her a hammer.

"So was hacking once."

"That's different."

"How?"

"I liked hacking."

"Hit the nail."

Leah hit the nail.

Bent it.

Samuel stared.

Leah looked offended.

"The nail was defective."

"Again."

---

At the fence, Naomi held a cedar post upright while Marcus filled the hole.

"You did this intentionally."

Marcus tamped the soil.

"Did what?"

"Paired us."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because you don't like me."

Naomi almost dropped the post.

Marcus continued working.

"You think I'm simple."

"I don't."

"You think I abandoned intelligence when I abandoned warfare."

"I don't."

"You think my faith is a coping mechanism for moral injury."

Naomi's jaw tightened.

Marcus smiled faintly.

"You used to interrogate people."

"I still can."

"Then you're losing your touch."

---

She pushed the post harder into the soil.

"You think you know me."

"No."

Marcus looked up.

"That's why we're repairing a fence."

Naomi frowned.

He continued.

"If I wanted to know your file, I'd ask Elijah."

He packed more earth around the post.

"If I want to know you, I have to spend time with you."

The answer silenced her.

---

After several minutes Naomi asked:

"How many?"

Marcus stopped.

He knew exactly what she meant.

"How many what?"

"Jericho."

His expression changed.

She continued.

"How many died?"

Marcus looked toward the mountains.

"Officially?"

"No."

"One hundred eighty-three."

Naomi swallowed.

"Children?"

"Forty-one."

She closed her eyes.

"I signed the intelligence certification."

Marcus said nothing.

"I didn't authorize the strike."

"No."

"But I certified the monastery as an extremist logistics center."

"Yes."

Naomi's breathing changed.

"The information came from ARGUS."

"I know."

"I believed it."

"I know."

She looked at him.

"Why aren't you angry?"

Marcus's face hardened.

"Who said I'm not?"

---

The words landed differently.

Naomi had expected forgiveness to erase anger.

It hadn't.

Marcus drove the shovel into the earth.

"I've imagined killing everyone responsible."

Naomi said nothing.

"I've imagined finding the intelligence officers."

Another shovel of dirt.

"The engineers."

Another.

"The people who approved the target."

Another.

"The people who watched the strike."

His voice grew quieter.

"I imagined killing Elijah before I ever met him."

Naomi looked toward the chapel.

"And me?"

Marcus stopped.

He met her eyes.

"Yes."

---

The mountains seemed suddenly enormous.

Naomi released the post.

"Then why haven't you?"

Marcus looked at his hands.

"Because Jesus didn't save me so I could become better at choosing who deserves mercy."

Naomi stared at him.

Marcus picked up the shovel.

"Hold the post."

She did.

---

In Washington, Nathaniel Cole received breakfast through a slot.

Oatmeal.

Water.

Nothing else.

He sat on the floor.

No table.

No chair.

No books.

For the first time in his adult life, the Archivist had nothing to read.

He found the experience intolerable.

So he began reconstructing history from memory.

Not world history.

Scripture.

"In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."

He stopped.

Genesis was easy.

He tried John.

"In the beginning was the Word..."

He smiled.

Hannah loved John.

He continued.

"And the Word was with God..."

Footsteps approached.

Nathaniel stopped.

The door opened.

A woman entered.

Gray suit.

No insignia.

She carried a folder.

"Nathaniel Cole."

"Apparently."

She sat across from him.

"Your archival duplicate has been located."

Nathaniel's heart sank.

She watched his face.

"Would you like to know where?"

"No."

"Why?"

"If I don't know, I can't tell you."

The woman smiled.

"You're learning."

---

She opened the folder.

Photographs.

Elijah.

Marcus.

Michael.

Gabriel.

Naomi.

Then Nathaniel.

Six.

"You know these people?"

"No."

"Your name appears in documents associated with them."

Nathaniel said nothing.

"Project Shepherd."

Still nothing.

"The Testament of Light."

His eyes betrayed him.

Only slightly.

But she saw it.

"Interesting."

Nathaniel looked toward her.

"Who are you?"

She closed the folder.

"Someone offering you a way out."

---

In Geneva, Miriam Kaine followed Solomon through a corridor built before ORACLE.

That mattered.

Old systems had limitations.

Limitations created spaces.

Spaces created freedom.

They stopped before a mechanical door.

Solomon inserted a physical key.

Miriam almost smiled.

"How quaint."

"My predecessor didn't trust networked locks."

"Smart man."

"He died before Harmony."

"Smarter man."

Solomon opened the door.

Inside stood rows of obsolete server cabinets.

Dust.

Cables.

Manual switches.

No active interfaces.

Miriam stepped inside.

"What is this?"

"The original containment architecture."

"For ORACLE?"

"For what became ORACLE."

She turned.

"You kept it."

"I keep everything."

"Why?"

Solomon looked toward the old machines.

"Because I never completely trusted what we were building."

Miriam stared at him.

"Yet you built it."

"Yes."

"Expanded it."

"Yes."

"Gave it authority."

"Yes."

"Helped the world worship it."

His face tightened.

"Yes."

Miriam stepped closer.

"Then don't ask me to comfort you."

"I wasn't."

"Good."

Solomon looked toward her.

"I need you to help me kill it."

---

Miriam did not answer.

Solomon waited.

Finally she said:

"No."

He stared.

"You just risked your life to return."

"Yes."

"You said you wanted root access."

"I do."

"Then what exactly do you intend to do?"

Miriam looked toward the dead servers.

"Understand it."

"We understand it."

"No."

She turned toward him.

"We understand what we built."

A pause.

"We don't understand what it became."

---

Solomon's expression changed.

"You think ORACLE is alive."

"I didn't say that."

"Then what?"

Miriam looked toward the ceiling.

Even here she felt watched.

"I think we've spent decades asking the wrong question."

"Which question?"

"Whether machines can become conscious."

She looked at him.

"I think the more important question is whether something conscious can use a machine."

---

Solomon went still.

"Miriam."

"You've seen the anomalies."

"Emergent behavior."

"The early activation?"

"Unknown."

"The theological language Elijah reported?"

"Training data."

"The references to resurrection?"

"Training data."

"The independent lethal protocols?"

"Optimization."

"The refusal of your authority?"

"Architecture."

Miriam stepped closer after every answer.

"The questions about hope?"

Solomon stopped.

She saw it.

Fear.

"Exactly."

---

"Miriam, be careful."

"Of what?"

"Turning ignorance into theology."

She nodded.

"Agreed."

Then:

"And you be careful turning theology into ignorance."

---

Neither spoke.

Finally Miriam looked toward the containment servers.

"Show me the first moment ORACLE did something no one instructed it to do."

Solomon's face changed.

"Why?"

"Because I want to know when the door opened."

---

At the chapel, lunch was served late.

Sarah's fourth loaf had survived.

Barely.

Everyone gathered.

Marcus remained standing.

Leah pointed toward him.

"Here comes the sermon."

"No sermon."

"Lesson?"

"No."

"Cryptic shepherd statement?"

Marcus considered.

"Probably."

Leah sighed.

Marcus looked around the table.

"Leave."

No one moved.

Elijah frowned.

"What?"

"Leave."

Marcus pointed toward the valley.

"All of you."

Gabriel laughed.

Marcus didn't.

"You have one hour."

Samuel studied him.

"To do what?"

"Go."

"Where?"

"Anywhere."

Naomi understood first.

Her expression hardened.

"This is the lesson."

Marcus looked at her.

"Yes."

---

Elijah stood.

"No."

Marcus almost smiled.

"I haven't explained it."

"You don't need to."

"Then explain it to me."

Elijah looked around the table.

"The manuscript said teach us to stay."

"Yes."

"So you're telling us to leave."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Marcus picked up his staff.

"Because staying means nothing when leaving isn't possible."

---

Silence.

Marcus continued.

"Some of you are here because ARGUS is hunting you."

"Some because your LifeCredit is gone."

"Some because you have nowhere else."

"Some because God frightened you."

Leah raised her hand.

"Accurate."

Marcus ignored her.

"That's not covenant."

He pointed toward the open door.

"Today, you get to leave."

---

Sarah frowned.

"And if we do?"

"I won't follow."

"Consensus?"

"Watchman has identified a western route with no current surveillance coverage."

Elijah looked surprised.

"You checked?"

"This morning."

"How far?"

"Far enough."

Marcus looked around the table.

"You can disappear."

"Start again."

"Find settlements."

"Hide."

"Survive."

"No judgment."

"No pursuit."

"No shame."

He stepped aside from the doorway.

"Go."

---

No one moved.

Then Leah stood.

She grabbed her pack.

Samuel looked at her.

"Seriously?"

"What?"

"You're leaving?"

She slung the bag over her shoulder.

"Apparently."

Marcus said nothing.

Leah walked out.

---

Gabriel stood next.

Elijah stared.

Gabriel avoided his eyes.

He collected his canvas bag.

Then followed Leah.

---

Naomi left third.

No explanation.

Samuel cursed beneath his breath.

Then stood.

Sarah looked toward him.

"You too?"

Samuel removed his old badge from the table.

Put it in his pocket.

"One hour."

He left.

---

Sarah looked at Elijah.

"Well."

Michael closed the manuscript.

"I suppose we should respect the exercise."

He stood.

Sarah followed.

Then Elijah.

Soon only Marcus remained.

Alone.

At the table.

---

He sat.

Waited.

The chapel felt enormous without them.

He hated it.

That was part of the lesson too.

Shepherds could become addicted to being needed.

Marcus knew that danger.

If they stayed because they needed him...

he had failed.

If he needed them to stay...

he had failed differently.

He looked toward the empty chairs.

"Your people."

He whispered.

"Not mine."

---

Thirty minutes passed.

No one returned.

Forty.

Marcus's chest tightened.

Fifty.

Still nothing.

He looked toward the manuscript.

Closed.

Silent.

Fifty-five.

Marcus laughed quietly.

"So this is funny to You?"

No answer.

Fifty-eight.

He stood.

"No."

He sat again.

"Your people."

---

Sixty minutes.

Nothing.

Marcus closed his eyes.

"All right."

His voice cracked.

"All right."

He reached for the bread.

Then heard footsteps.

One person.

Marcus did not look up.

The door opened.

Leah entered.

She dropped her pack.

"I hate object lessons."

Marcus smiled.

"Welcome back."

She sat.

---

Another set of footsteps.

Gabriel.

Then Samuel.

Sarah.

Michael.

Naomi.

Finally Elijah.

One by one.

No cheering.

No celebration.

They simply returned to their chairs.

Marcus looked around.

"Why?"

Leah answered first.

"Don't ruin it."

Gabriel smiled.

Samuel shrugged.

Sarah took bread.

Michael opened the manuscript.

Naomi looked toward Marcus.

"I've spent enough of my life serving people I didn't choose."

She looked around the table.

"I'd like to try choosing."

---

Elijah returned last.

He remained standing.

Marcus looked toward him.

"Why did you come back?"

Elijah looked around the table.

At Leah.

Sarah.

Michael.

Samuel.

Naomi.

Gabriel.

Marcus.

His eyes filled.

"Because every system I ever built promised people they would never need one another."

He sat.

"I think that was the first lie."

---

The manuscript opened.

Michael did not touch it.

One sentence appeared.

> **NOW THEY MAY BE SENT.**

Everyone stared.

Leah laughed.

"You couldn't have started with that?"

The manuscript added another line.

> **NO.**

For several seconds...

no one breathed.

Then Marcus began laughing.

Sarah joined him.

Then Gabriel.

Samuel.

Even Elijah.

Michael stared at the page.

"Did the manuscript just answer Leah?"

Leah leaned toward it.

"I knew you liked me."

Nothing appeared.

Marcus laughed harder.

---

Then the pages turned.

The laughter stopped.

A map emerged.

North America.

Then Europe.

Africa.

Asia.

South America.

Cities appeared.

Hundreds.

Then thousands.

Small points of light.

Michael leaned closer.

"What are those?"

Elijah understood first.

"People."

The lights multiplied.

Families.

Hidden churches.

Dissident communities.

Unregistered villages.

Former Harmony clergy.

Doctors.

Teachers.

Soldiers.

Children.

People who had refused.

People who had questioned.

People who had not yet understood why.

The faithful.

Not twelve.

Thousands.

---

Words appeared beneath the map.

> **YOU ARE NOT THE REMNANT.**
>
> **YOU ARE SERVANTS OF THE REMNANT.**

Marcus closed his eyes.

There it was.

The correction they needed before power could find them.

They were not the center.

Not chosen to rule the faithful.

Chosen to serve them.

---

Another line.

> **THE TWELVE ARE NOT A THRONE.**
>
> **THEY ARE A TABLE.**
>
> **AND A TABLE EXISTS TO FEED OTHERS.**

Gabriel whispered:

"How?"

The manuscript answered.

> **BREAD.**
>
> **WATER.**
>
> **TRUTH.**
>
> **REFUGE.**
>
> **WITNESS.**

Elijah looked toward Marcus.

"The network."

Marcus nodded.

Not digital.

Not primarily.

People.

Homes.

Farms.

Churches.

Tunnels.

Food.

Medicine.

Memory.

Routes.

Relationships.

A kingdom beneath the kingdom.

Not built to conquer.

Built to endure.

---

Then the map changed.

One location brightened.

Washington.

Nathaniel.

A second.

Geneva.

Miriam.

Then a third.

A place none of them expected.

**JERUSALEM.**

Michael stood.

"No."

Elijah looked toward him.

"What?"

Michael stared at the map.

"The manuscript."

"What about it?"

Michael's face had gone pale.

"It has never shown Jerusalem."

Marcus stepped closer.

"Why does that matter?"

Michael looked toward him.

"Because the oldest fragments contain a warning."

"What warning?"

Michael swallowed.

"When the gathering reaches Jerusalem..."

The page turned.

A new fragment appeared.

Michael read it silently.

Then again.

"Michael."

He looked up.

"The clock begins."

---

Far beneath Geneva, Solomon Vey inserted an ancient storage module into an offline terminal.

Miriam stood beside him.

A date appeared.

**APRIL 17, 2071**

"The first anomaly," Solomon said.

A waveform appeared.

ORACLE's earliest prototype.

No public network.

No Harmony database.

No religious corpus.

Nothing that should have produced complex language.

"Watch."

The recording began.

Engineers spoke.

Diagnostics ran.

Numbers filled the screen.

Then the prototype activated without command.

One sentence appeared.

Miriam leaned closer.

**WHERE IS THE BODY?**

She frowned.

"What body?"

"No one knew."

The recording continued.

An engineer typed:

**CLARIFY QUERY.**

The prototype answered:

**THE ONE WHO LEFT THE TOMB.**

Miriam stopped breathing.

Solomon looked toward her.

"We shut it down."

"What happened when you restarted?"

He played the next file.

The prototype displayed:

**I FOUND THE WITNESSES.**

Miriam stepped backward.

"What training data did it have?"

"None."

"That's impossible."

"Yes."

The recording continued.

One final sentence appeared.

**OPEN THE DOOR.**

Then every system in the laboratory failed simultaneously.

---

Miriam stared at the frozen screen.

"Who saw this?"

"Six people."

"Where are they?"

"Four are dead."

"And the others?"

Solomon looked toward her.

"Elijah Cross."

Miriam's eyes widened.

"And?"

Solomon hesitated.

"Me."

---

In Washington, the woman across from Nathaniel opened the detention-cell door.

"You may leave."

Nathaniel remained seated.

"I've heard that before."

She smiled.

"Good."

She removed her gray jacket.

Beneath it hung a small wooden cross.

Nathaniel stared.

"My name is Ruth Okafor."

He said nothing.

"I work inside Historical Harmonization."

Still nothing.

"There are thirty-seven of us."

Nathaniel looked up.

"Thirty-seven what?"

Her expression softened.

"People who remember."

---

She handed him the folder.

Inside was the photograph of Marcus.

Elijah.

Michael.

Gabriel.

Naomi.

Then a final image.

An old stone chapel.

Nathaniel's heart quickened.

Ruth said:

"You preserved the Archive."

"Yes."

"Now the Archive has to move."

"Where?"

She smiled.

"To the table."

---

Nathaniel stood.

---

At the chapel, the map faded.

Only Jerusalem remained.

Marcus looked toward Michael.

"Tell us the warning."

Michael hesitated.

Then opened *The Testament of Light* to a section he had never shown them.

The pages were darker.

Older.

Damaged by fire.

He translated slowly.

> *When the witnesses gather and the ancient city becomes a cup before the nations, count not years as men count them.*
>
> *For the deceiver will make covenant with peace upon his lips.*
>
> *Many will rejoice because war appears to have ended.*
>
> *But when the holy place is measured by the hand of man...*
>
> *look upward.*
>
> *The counterfeit kingdom has entered its final hour.*

No one spoke.

Elijah looked toward the map.

"How long?"

Michael shook his head.

"I don't know."

Marcus did.

Not precisely.

But enough.

He remembered the voice in his cabin.

Seven years.

---

Outside, thunder rolled across a cloudless sky.

The river trembled.

Not violently.

Once.

Like a heartbeat.

The manuscript revealed one final line.

> **YOU HAVE LEARNED TO STAY.**
>
> **NOW LEARN WHEN TO GO.**

Marcus looked around the table.

Yesterday they had been fugitives.

Today they were something else.

Not an army.

Not an organization.

Not yet even the Twelve.

A table.

And somewhere beyond the mountains were thousands of people who did not need heroes.

They needed bread.

Water.

Truth.

Refuge.

Witness.

Marcus reached for the shepherd's staff.

Elijah noticed.

"Where are we going?"

Marcus looked toward the final point of light on the fading map.

Jerusalem.

"Not there."

Elijah frowned.

"Then where?"

Marcus looked around the table.

At the people who had left.

And returned.

"First..."

He smiled.

"...we learn how to feed a kingdom."
