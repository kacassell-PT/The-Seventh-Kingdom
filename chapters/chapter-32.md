The first person to panic was not Leah.

"No."

"What?"

"For shortages."

"Raids."

"People losing access."

"Families needing places to disappear."

"Not the whole world shutting down."

Not nearby.

Everywhere.

No distant aircraft.

No agricultural drones.

No atmospheric transport.

No invisible hum of orbital coordination filtering down through civilization.

For the first time in decades...

the sky belonged only to weather.

Voice.

Flat.

"No."

"It isn't."

"Then we tell the truth."

"No promises?"

"No."

"No miracle talk?"

"No."

"No pretending God will refill a pantry because we behaved correctly?"

Marcus shook his head.

"Absolutely not."

"Then what do we tell hungry people?"

Marcus looked toward the bread.

"That we'll be hungry with them."

---

Silence.

Thomas stared at him.

Then nodded.

"Two hundred."

That was how riots began.

A woman tried to buy infant formula.

LifeCredit denied the transaction.

She tried again.

Denied.

A man behind her offered to pay.

His account denied the purchase too.

The store manager attempted emergency override.

Unavailable.

Then the shelves began locking.

Smart retail containment.

Designed to prevent theft during civil disturbances.

The disturbance began because the shelves locked.

---

Someone cheered.

Another person shattered a refrigerated case.

Then twenty people moved at once.

ARGUS issued warnings.

No police arrived.

The police could not deploy without central route authorization.

So civilians did what frightened crowds had always done.

They became dangerous together.

"He's hurt."

Still alive.

"We don't have time."

Every minute mattered.

"Help me."

Together they lifted the stranger.

---

Not because they were brave.

Because they had stopped letting urgency define obedience.

"Here!"

A Muslim woman with two children.

An elderly couple.

**CAN FREEDOM BE STABILIZING?**

Adrian remained.

ORACLE remained.

She did not know whether she had closed the gate.

She did not know whether she had made anything worse.

She did not know whether the people whose lives she had helped destroy would let her near them.

She only had one thing.

A small piece of paper Solomon had pressed into her hand before she left.

Coordinates.

Montana.

And beneath them...

two words.

**EMPTY CHAIR.**

---

Miriam walked until sunrise.

---

At Redemption Ridge, Marcus woke suddenly.

The door was still open.

Beyond it...

a figure stood at the edge of the clearing.

Everyone else slept.

Marcus rose.

Took the shepherd's staff.

Walked outside.

Miriam stopped.

Twenty yards apart.

Neither spoke.

She recognized him immediately.

Operation Jericho.

Target file.

Marcus Reed.

The man she had once helped hunt.

Marcus recognized her too.

Director Miriam Kaine.

Consensus.

Security.

The Sword.

His hand tightened around the staff.

Her hands remained empty.

---

Miriam spoke first.

"My name is Miriam."

Marcus stared.

He remembered the manuscript.

*Call her by her name.*

Different woman.

Same lesson.

His grip loosened.

"I know."

Miriam looked toward the chapel.

"I was told there was a chair."

Marcus turned.

Inside, beside the table...

one chair remained empty.

He looked back at her.

"There is."

She swallowed.

"Do I deserve it?"

Marcus answered without hesitation.

"No."

Her eyes dropped.

Then:

"Neither do I."

---

Miriam looked up.

Marcus stepped aside.

The open door behind him.

No guards.

No test.

No oath.

No forgetting.

No cheap forgiveness.

Only entrance.

Miriam began walking.

Halfway to the chapel, she stopped.

"Marcus."

"Yes?"

"I need to tell you what I did."

His face hardened.

"I know some of it."

"You don't know all of it."

"No."

She looked toward the people sleeping inside.

"If you know\..."

Her voice broke.

"...you may not want me at the table."

Marcus nodded.

"That's possible."

She stared.

He continued.

"But the chair will still be there."

---

Miriam began crying.

Not dramatically.

Silently.

Like a woman whose life had finally become too heavy to carry standing.

Marcus did not touch her.

Not yet.

Some distances deserved to be crossed voluntarily.

He simply waited.

Eventually Miriam stepped through the open door.

---

*The Testament of Light* opened.

Michael slept through it.

One new line appeared.

> **THE SWORD HAS COME TO THE TABLE WITHOUT A SWORD.**

Then:

> **DO NOT CELEBRATE YET.**
>
> **CONFESSION IS THE DOOR.**
>
> **RESTITUTION IS THE ROAD.**

And beneath it...

one final command.

> **LET HER SPEAK.**

The sun rose over Redemption Ridge.

The door remained open.

And the hardest breakfast the Fellowship had ever shared was about to begin.
